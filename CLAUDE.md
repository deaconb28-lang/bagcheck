# Bagcheck

Fitness tracking for your investment portfolio. Read-only, permanently: Bagcheck describes what the user did — it never places trades, never recommends one, never alerts on price.

Two faces, one product. Inside, Bagcheck is a dark instrument that scores behaviour. Outside, it sells the artefact those numbers mint: the public angle is Wrapped-first — **"Turn your portfolio into a flex"** — connect a brokerage in two taps, get a year worth posting. The score is introduced on the landing as the coming-soon second act ("Whoop for your portfolio"), behind a waitlist. The brand is the lowercase wordmark **bagcheck** and the bag mark — a filled bag with an amber tick struck through it — drawn once as `BagMark` in `app/(marketing)/BagMark.tsx` and mirrored as the favicon in `app/icon.svg` and the home-screen icon in `app/apple-icon.png`. Keep the three in step; Next serves `apple-icon` as a raster only, so that one is a PNG rendered from the same geometry. The wordmark is never capitalised in UI chrome (prose may still say Bagcheck).

Reference docs (read before any UI work — the design system is authoritative on anything visual):

- `docs/design-system/` — the retired Kylani v0.8 paper-and-ink handoff, kept as history. **The visual authority is now this file's Colour/Type/Layout sections plus `styles/tokens.css`** — the dark luminous system the share cards established.
- `docs/bagcheck-feature-architecture.md` — product layers. Its brand addendum is superseded by the design system above.
- `docs/bagcheck-build-instructions.md` — stack, project shape, milestones
- `docs/bagcheck-unit-economics.md` — what a month costs and what conversion rate the pricing needs. SnapTrade is 83% of marginal cost and is billed on free users; break-even at $9/$29 is ~10%. `docs/costs.mjs` re-runs the model.
- `docs/bagcheck-ai-architecture.md` — **authoritative on the AI boundary**: the five tiers, the decision procedure before any model call, the SnapTrade→Mongo→screen layers, the fact-pack contract, and the six touchpoints that earn a model. Read it before adding one.
- `.claude/skills/` — seven design skills vendored verbatim from the MIT-licensed [ui-ux-pro-max](https://github.com/nextlevelbuilder/ui-ux-pro-max-skill) plugin, with `ui-ux-pro-max` the one this project leans on: a searchable database of styles, palettes, font pairings and UX rules. Consult it before a visual decision — the app's dark field and the share cards' poster grammar both came out of it. **Never edit those files**; they are a copy, and `.claude/skills/UPSTREAM.md` records the pinned version and how to update.

## Colour

- `styles/tokens.css` is the single source of colour. A palette hex anywhere else in `app/`, `components/`, `lib/`, or `styles/` is a bug. Always `var(--*)`; when a value needs alpha, `color-mix(in srgb, var(--token) N%, transparent)`. (`docs/` is imported reference material and exempt.)
- **White and dark. Nothing else, except where a colour means something — and white is the base.** The app was dark-only, which made every screen a night instrument whether or not anyone was reading it at night, and a ledger is a document before it is a cockpit. Light is now `:root`; dark is `html[data-mode="dark"]`, a mode rather than the identity. `app/layout.tsx` ships `data-mode="light"` and `themeColor: "#f4f4f6"`. Everything downstream reads `var(--*)` and never learns which mode it is in — a rule that names a mode is a rule in the wrong file.
- **`<ModeSwitch>` on the rail is the only way to flip it** (`components/app/ModeSwitch.tsx`). It writes `document.documentElement.dataset.mode`, remembers the choice in `localStorage["bagcheck-mode"]` so a reload does not wait on a round trip, and posts to `/api/prefs/mode` best-effort — a preference that fails to save must never interrupt the person who set it.
- Canvas `--bg` (#f4f4f6), panel and rail pure white `--s1`/`--sunken`, chip fill `--inset`, tile `--tile`. **A panel is a white plate on a near-white ground, held by a 1px `--edge` and the softest possible lift** (`--lift`; `--lift-hero` under the hero). Rows inside a list divide with `--line-light`; controls outline with `--line2`. No heavy borders, no glass, no bevel — and the ground is never pure white, or a plate has nothing to sit on.
- Five families, each with one job. Hue carries meaning and never variety, and **each family is stated twice — a light value and a dark one — because a hue legible on #0B0B12 is unreadable on white**:
  - `--moss` (light #0f7d44, dark #4ADE80, green) — **money up, and nothing else.** It used to mean discipline as well, which put a green ring over a green eyebrow over four green meters over four more in the archetype panel over a hundred green heat cells, all on one screen — and a dashboard whose dominant colour is one hue is a dashboard where the hue has stopped carrying information. Green is now a positive figure in dollars: the P&L total, a green session, a positive impact.
  - `--loss` (light #c62f37, dark #F26D6D, red) — negative P&L and literally nothing else. A clear red, never a siren; the minus sign and the word still ride along.
  - `--signal` (light #5b6672, dark #A4B0BD, cooled steel) — exposure, comparison, percentile, a deliberate step down. Also the Plus tier's mark.
  - `--ember` (light #a8500a, dark #F59E0B, amber) — streaks, records, scarcity and the live.
  - `--accent` (light #6d3fd4, dark #A78BFA, violet) — **reserved: Bagcheck's own voice**, which is now everything the product *concluded* rather than everything that went right. The written daily insight, the score and its ring, the consistency ramp (`--h1`–`--h4`), Wrapped identity, Investor Age. Never a hover fill, never decoration.
- **The light values are set by contrast, not by taste.** A figure often sits *inside* its own tint, so it has to clear 4.5:1 against that tint rather than against white: `--meta` #6e6e78 is 4.9:1 on white, `--moss` #0f7d44 is 5.1:1, `--ember` #a8500a is 4.8:1 on `--ember-tint`. Mono labels are 10–12px, which WCAG counts as small text, so the quietest grey in the system still has to clear 4.5. Darkening a hue for light mode means darkening its `-tint`, `-line`, `-glow` and `-halo` with it.
- **A hue never fills a surface, never paints chrome, and never marks a hover.** It reaches a figure, a 3px meter, a 1px semantic edge, and stops. If a screen looks colourful, something took a colour it did not earn. The rail's active tab is `--ink-fill`, not a hue — navigation furniture is the clearest case of chrome there is.
- **The score component meters are `signal`, all four.** They were moss, moss, moss, signal; the figure beside each is what carries the reading, and four saturated bars in a hero plus four more in the archetype panel was the largest single block of colour in the product.
- **A density grid is a texture, not a fill.** `HeatGrid` caps its cells at 15px and scrolls past its panel rather than stretching them: a hundred-odd cells at full panel width become 24px tiles and the panel reads as one saturated block, which is the same failure whatever hue it is in.
- **Glow is rationed to live data, and in light it is not a glow at all.** `--glow-*` and `--halo-*` mark a key metric that is measured and current — the score ring, the sync dot, an active streak. On the dark field they are light; on white the same tokens resolve to a 1px ring and a wash at 5% that you notice only by its absence. One halo per surface (`.hero` takes `data-halo`). Never chrome, never a heading, never a hover state.
- The strongest object in a view is the primary action: `--ink-field` — near-black on white, white on dark. The inversion is the strongest gesture available either way, so it is spent on exactly one thing per screen.
- The drifting ridge artwork behind the shell is **dark-only**: `app/(app)/app.module.css` hides `.field` and shows it under `html[data-mode="dark"]`. At any opacity that reads as texture on black it reads as a smudge on white.
- Status is never encoded by colour alone: every state carries a word. Five hues with one meaning each only works if the meaning is also written down.
- Share cards keep the `--share-*` and `--card-*` families and their hue art, declared once on `:root` and **never flipped by mode** — a card is an artefact with its own palette, saturated well past anything the app uses, and an artefact that changed colour with the reader's preference would not be one artefact. That gap is the editorial contrast rather than a leak.
- **The marketing field is the second world, and it turns over once: a white editorial hero, then a dark field that runs unbroken from the Wrapped deck to the footer.** It is spoken entirely in `--mk-*` tokens, which live in `styles/tokens.css` and are read only by `app/(marketing)`; the app's tokens never appear on the landing and `--mk-*` never enters the app. The hero is black ink on white. Everything below it sits on `--mk-field`, white ink, with `--mk-violet-soft` for eyebrows, `--mk-green-soft` for money-up, `--mk-red-soft` for money-down and `--mk-amber` for the live and the scarce. Full-strength `--mk-green`/`--mk-red` are reserved for the outliers in a chart, so the ordinary days sit back. Violet and amber gradients belong to the artefacts — Wrapped cards, the early-access tier — never to a section's own ground. The phone mocks are `aria-hidden` illustrations of the app, built in JSX, never screenshots.
- Tone props follow the tokens: `moss`, `signal`, `ember`, `clay`, `accent`, `neutral`, `ink`. Contributor tones are persisted in Mongo, so read them through `contributorTone()` — pre-existing documents still carry the old `gold`/`violet` spellings.

## Type — one voice for the instrument, voices for the cards

- **General Sans** 400–700 is the product's voice everywhere it speaks: sentences, buttons, inputs, headings, and hero figures. `.disp` takes it at 600.
- **Space Grotesk** 400–700 is now only `--font-display`/`.num` — the tabular figures inside rows and tables, where a column has to line up. `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums`. Never a sentence, never a label.
- **Public Sans** remains the fallback under General Sans and the face the share cards set their ledes in.
- **JetBrains Mono** 400/500/600 owns labels, eyebrows, timestamps, counts and comparatives. Never body copy, never a heading. The moment a metric label renders in the body face the system smears.
- **Card-only voices:** Anton (`--font-poster`) and Playfair Display (`--font-serif`) exist solely on share cards, where a template is a voice — they never appear in app chrome. Enforced by scope: the variables are only read inside `components/cards`, `app/c` and `app/og`.
- Hierarchy comes from size contrast, not new elements: the number huge in the grotesque, the label tiny in mono, the tail a quiet body line. If a card reads flat, widen the gap between number and label — do not add a weight, a colour or an element.
- Three text colours: `--ink` for figures and headlines, `--ink3` for sentences and tails, `--meta` for mono metadata (`--ink2` for mid emphasis where a row needs it).
- **General Sans is the product's voice, declared once**, in `app/layout.tsx` as `--font-voice`, and read by all three groups. Headings and hero figures take it at 600–700, which is what makes a score read like the year on the Wrapped screen. `.num` keeps Space Grotesk with `tabular-nums`, because a ledger column has to line up and that is the whole job of that class. Mono is unchanged. Never Roboto, never Inter.
- **Every face is vendored in `public/fonts` and served from our own origin — nothing is fetched from a CDN, at build time or at runtime.** This is not tidiness. `next/font/google` downloads its woff2 files *during `next build`*, so one bad minute at `fonts.gstatic.com` fails the build; it did, on CI run 31859584940, with "Failed to fetch `Playfair Display`". And General Sans was a `<link>` to `api.fontshare.com` duplicated in three group layouts, which put a third party in the critical render path of every page and handed it every reader's IP — while `/legal/privacy` claims nothing of the sort is there. `npm run fonts` refreshes the files; the paths in `layout.tsx` must stay written-out literals, because `next/font/local` parses that call statically and rejects a shared constant. Satori takes TTF/OTF/WOFF and **cannot read woff2**, which is why `outfit-800.ttf` sits alongside the rest and why share cards had been silently setting their type in a fallback face.

## Screen structure

- Every screen is score-first: `<ScreenHeader>` sticky at the top, then a hero number and its decomposition. Nothing above the fold is prose.
- **Home is a payments dashboard and opens like one: a summary row, then Wrapped, then the score.** Four figures across one plate divided by hairlines (`home.module.css .kpis`) — realised P&L, discipline, streak, ledger — because a reader opening the dashboard wants to know how the account is doing before anything else, and four numbers in a row answer that in the height of one panel. Boxing each of them separately would be four small filled rectangles inside a big one, which is the tell this file already warns about.
- **Wrapped is a panel on the dashboard, not a route you have to know about.** `<WrappedPanel>` sits directly under the summary row with the year, the earned count, the first five cards and one door; the deck, its locked frames and the player stay on `/wrapped`. It is the product's most shareable surface and it was the least-visited screen in it. It costs no extra read: `assembleWrappedFrom(data, opens)` takes the screen the page has already loaded, and `assembleWrapped(userId)` is the thin loading wrapper the other callers use. `loadScreen` defaults to the same 400 scores the assembly needs, so the two were literally re-reading one document.
- `<ScreenHeader>` carries a conversational title over a mono line of machine facts, then the account cluster: the sync pill, the tier chip and the one upgrade button. The score rides it on every route so the number never leaves the screen — except on Home, which passes `score={null}` because the ring and the figure are the first thing on the page and a third copy in the chrome is noise. **Investor Age** (computed in `lib/score/age.ts`, deterministic, bounded 18–72, exposure deliberately absent) reads violet wherever it appears.
- **`app/(flex)` is the Wrapped flow and it is not one of the seven — it is its own world.** `/start` and `/wrapped` speak the marketing field and General Sans, carry no rail, and sit outside the launch lock, because the landing hands off to them directly and a signed-out visitor has to be able to reach them.
- **`/wrapped` opens on the deck, it runs edge to edge, and it is always the full set.** Two builds got this wrong in opposite directions: a snap rail that showed one and a half cards on a desktop and navigated by thirteen anonymous dashes, then a cover-plus-grid contact sheet that fixed the navigation and broke the impression — on a real account that had earned two cards it rendered one hero, one lonely tile under a heading reading "1 more card", and a thousand pixels of empty field, on the one screen whose whole job is *look how much you got*. `<Deck>` is horizontal, snap-scrolled, full-bleed, and carries every kind in `ROSTER` order: earned frames hold their card, unearned ones hold their name and **the one condition that mints them** (`lib/cards/roster.ts`, pure and tested). Nothing is invented — a locked frame states a requirement, never a figure — and `deckFrames` appends any card the roster has not caught up with rather than dropping it.
- The deck's inset is `--deck-inset`, an absolute expression rather than the usual `50vw - 50%` trick, because `padding` resolves percentages against the containing block and `scroll-padding` against the scrollport — which after the full-bleed margin *is* the viewport, so the shorthand collapses to zero there and the browser snaps card one flush to the window edge.
- The frame number is the structural device and it is load-bearing: the deck, the player and the roster share one order, and closing the player returns the reader to the frame they stopped on. Play opens on `firstEarned`, never on a locked frame.
- **Everything gated carries a drawing of what is behind the gate.** `components/cards/Teaser.tsx` holds one closed vocabulary of drawn readouts — a ring with a gap, four meters, a distribution with a marker, a fanned set of cards — and both surfaces that gate something read from it: the unearned frames in the deck (`ROSTER[n].teaser`) and the roadmap rows. **Every drawing is shapes and carries no numerals.** A teaser with a plausible figure on it would undermine every real figure beside it, on the screen whose whole claim is that its numbers came off a brokerage: a ring with a gap says *a score*, it does not say 82. They are drawn rather than fetched for the same reason the rail's route glyphs are — they must be on the first paint and they take `currentColor`. `--teaser-lit` is the one lit colour in a drawing and the caller sets it: violet where the thing is Bagcheck's own reading, green where it is money, amber where it is scarce.
- **The roadmap under the deck is a list, not seventeen cards.** `<ComingSoon>` used to give every item the full card treatment — border, blurred drawn readout, lock glyph, name, sentence, status — seventeen times down four bands, which is a wall: nothing is scannable because nothing is distinguishable, and the ghost readouts made a section about *not having data yet* look dense with data. Four groups, one hairline-ruled row each with its own teaser drawing, a status word from a closed set of three, and a small tally of those three. Seventeen rows survive being a list only because every drawing is different — the tiled build reused six shapes across seventeen tiles, which is what made it read as one component repeated — and each group lights its drawings in its own hue so the eye can tell at a glance which section it is in. No dates, ever: a shipped date is a promise that file cannot keep.
- **Setup ends in the dashboard.** `/start`'s linked state and `<FirstRun>`'s finished state both hand over to `/home` rather than leaving the reader on whichever screen the brokerage portal happened to return to. The greeting and first read still happen on `/wrapped`, because that is where the portal lands and the cards filling in underneath is the whole point of it — the door appears once the sync is done.
- **Signing in is one click from anywhere.** `<GoogleSignIn>` defaults to `redirectTo="/home"` and appears in the landing nav and the Wrapped bar as well as on the signed-out app screens. The nav used to link to `/home`, which for a signed-out visitor is a screen whose entire content is another sign-in button.
- **Three routes: `/home`, `/wrapped`, `/you`.** There were seven, and between them they held maybe two pages of content and a great deal of repetition — the archetype rendered on three, the components on two, and a third of the tiles were locked panels showing invented figures. The split was wrong in the first place: `/home` answers *how is it going*, `/wrapped` is the artefact, and everything else is one question — *what does my own history say about me* — which does not need four tabs to ask. `/dna`, `/patterns`, `/insights` and `/cards` are `permanentRedirect`s into `/you`; `/ledger` is a plain 307 because the ledger may return. `/today`, `/portfolio`, `/activity` and `/reports` still redirect too — they are in every bookmark and every link minted before the rename. **Every redirect stub carries `export const dynamic = "force-dynamic"`**: they were statically prerendered, which baked the locked layout's `redirect("/")` into them at build time, so all of them had been answering with the marketing page. `/profile` is settings, reachable from the avatar, and is not a tab.
- **`/you` is five blocks and nothing invented**: portfolio value with the equity curve, the holdings table, the archetype with the four components as figures, the findings (absent rather than empty when nothing clears a sample floor), the minted cards, and the paid formats. It reads `data.derived.findings` rather than scanning.
- One hero panel per screen. Row dividers inside a panel are `--line-light`, and the last row drops its border.

## Layout and surfaces

- 76px icon rail (`components/app/AppRail.tsx`), `--sunken`, sticky full height, three 46px glyph buttons with native `title` tooltips — do not build a tooltip system. `<ModeSwitch>` then the avatar at the foot. Below 900px it becomes the same three as a bottom bar, which is why `MOBILE_ROUTES` is now literally `ROUTES`: it existed because seven 48px targets across 390px is a tab bar you mis-tap, and three is not.
- **Reading screens are one centred column capped at 760px; Home is a dashboard and takes 1240px.** The narrow measure is right for a document — DNA, Insights, Patterns, the Ledger — and wrong for the one screen whose job is to show several readings at once: a 760px column centred in a 1440px window left a third of the display empty on either side and pushed everything actionable below the fold. Home widens (`home.module.css .wide`) and lays its panels on a two-column grid where each says for itself whether it needs both (`.span`).
- **Panels read from the left.** They used to centre, which suited a single narrow column of one-line statements and broke the moment two panels sat side by side — a centred lockup floating in a half-width panel has no edge to start from and the eye has to find the beginning of every line twice. Data rows stay left-aligned for the same reason they always did.
- **Home opens with a lockup, not a stack.** The dial on the left, the score enormous beside it, the delta, the written sentence, then the four components across the foot under a hairline — the same grammar `/wrapped` opens with and the landing sells. The ring is drawn `bare` there: the figure is already set at 100px three inches away, and a ring printing it again is the same measurement twice. The readings that used to crowd the header (streak, Investor Age, patterns on file, longest run) sit on the page as four linked tiles, because a fact about the reader belongs on the page and the header should not have to carry it on all seven routes.
- Flex and grid with `gap` only. No margin-spaced inline siblings. Fixed-count grids use `minmax(0, 1fr)`.
- **Radii are large and the type is big, because the plates are quiet.** 40 hero, 32 panel, 28 card-in-grid, 26 tiles and nested surfaces, 18 rows, 14 buttons, 12 controls, 999 pills. On a white plate held by a hairline there is no border weight doing the shaping, so the corner is what gives a surface its character — a 12px radius on a 40px-padded plate reads as a form field.
- **Air is the point.** 48px page padding, 40px panel padding, 34px between panels in the dash grid, 26px inside a panel, 140px at the foot. Everything still sits on a 4px base — 4/8/12/16/20/24/26/32/40/48 — and an off-grid value is a bug, not a taste call. If a screen feels crowded the fix is to take a panel off it, not to shave the gaps.
- **A surface is a fill plus a 1px `--edge` plus `--lift`.** Hairlines inside a panel stay rationed: row dividers (`--line-light`) and the rule above a footer. Semantic chip borders (`--moss-line`, `--signal-line`, `--ember-line`, `--accent-line`, `--loss-line`) carry meaning. Resting elevation is the softest shadow that separates a plate from its ground and nothing more — never grey, never coloured, never a second shadow layer.
- **A stat is not a box.** Eyebrow, number, 3px meter, tail, laid out as a plain column in a `gap` grid. Four small filled rectangles inside a big one is the tell.
- Blur is chrome, not style: the sticky `ScreenHeader` frosts over the canvas, and `filter: blur()` appears on a locked tile. Nowhere else. No bevels, no inner shadows, no glass panels.
- The one gradient in the app is the fade over the drifting field in `app/(app)/app.module.css`, which only paints in dark mode, and its whole job is to push the artwork almost out of sight.
- 44px hit targets on mobile; interactive rows ≥40px. Err toward whitespace.
- **The phone reads from the left too.** Below 780px the hero stacks — dial, then figure, then sentence — and stays left-aligned, because a centred lockup over a column of left-aligned panels makes the phone look like a different screen from the desktop. The dial halves there (`--ring-size` on `.heroRing`; `ScoreRing` reads `var(--ring-size, var(--size))` so a surface can ask for less than the caller did) rather than spending a third of the fold on a circle that carries no number.

## Data idioms

- One idiom per statement, each legible at 200px wide.
- Full equity curves render on `/dna` only. Never four identical sparklines.
- Screens read `lib/db/derived.ts`, they do not scan. Round trips, daily P&L, equity and hold times are materialised once per sync — four screens each rebuilding every round trip per navigation is a timeout waiting for a big enough ledger. Bump `DERIVED_VERSION` when that file changes meaning.
- A name whose FIFO-implied units disagree with the position snapshot is excluded from statistics, never silently averaged in. The position still renders; only the inference stops.
- No decorative gradients and no emoji.
- Icons are a closed vocabulary, not a set. `lib/icons/names.ts` `ICONS` is the whole list, and a new glyph means a new line in it — `/api/icon` serves names, never search terms. They label a fixed taxonomy — the ledger kinds, the three tiers, and the five situations an empty state can be in — and never decorate a heading, a button or a card.
- An empty state's icon names the *situation*, not the screen: signed out, deployment unconfigured, no brokerage, nothing synced, nothing scored yet. The same predicament looks the same on all seven screens.
- `iconsEnabled()` is a server-side read — the key is not `NEXT_PUBLIC` and must not become one. A client component asking it always gets `false`, so a screen asks once and passes the answer down as a prop.
- The rail's route glyphs in `components/app/routes.tsx` are drawn inline by us and stay that way. They are chrome — they must be on the first paint of every screen and take `currentColor` so the active state is one background swap. A fetched glyph would be a downgrade and a dependency.
- An `<Icon>` is painted as a CSS mask, never an `<img>` or inline SVG: the shape comes from the API and the colour is `currentColor`, so a glyph takes the token of the row it sits in and no palette hex enters from outside `styles/tokens.css`.

## Stat card grammar

- Every card reads label → number → unit → why, enforced by `<Stat>` prop order: `eyebrow → value → unit → tail`.
- The eyebrow names the metric the number measures — never an event name over a percentage.
- The tail always ships a specific comparison, never generic encouragement.

## Motion

Motion is the one warm gesture in an instrument aesthetic. The softening happens in time rather than in ornament — do not add a decorative element to compensate.

- Global transition on buttons and inputs: `background, border-color, color, box-shadow` at .16s ease.
- **Scroll-driven reveals, not a load stagger.** Sections carry `data-reveal` and the `rise` keyframe; one rule in `globals.css` retargets them onto the scroll position where the browser supports it. Content above the fold is past its range at load, so it is simply there.
- Meters use `scaleX` at 800ms staggered 70ms; wave bars use `scaleY` staggered 8ms per bar so the chart draws left to right.
- The ring renders at the full-circle `stroke-dashoffset` and gets its real offset in an effect, so CSS transitions it. **The static state must be correct if the effect never runs** — under reduced motion the offset is set immediately.
- A mode change crossfades over .42s via `[data-surface] *`. Flipping light/dark should feel like a dimmer, not a repaint. It also means a contrast probe taken sooner than that measures mid-flight colours.
- Hover: rail items −1px, trophy cards −6px with −.7deg, heat cells scale 1.35, the mode toggle rotates 45°. Panels do **not** lift on hover — with borders gone there is nothing to lift.
- Everything honours `prefers-reduced-motion`, and JS tweens check `matchMedia` before starting.
- Marketing may use scroll-driven motion (`animation-timeline: view()` / `scroll()`), always behind `@supports` and `prefers-reduced-motion`, and always so the static state is the finished design. Never write a hover rule against a property a scroll animation also writes — `fill: both` wins and the hover silently dies.
- Ease-out, nothing over 500ms — for *arrivals*. Ambient loops (`breathe`, `floaty`, `wander`, `shimmer`) run long and slow, and are allowed in exactly two places: light behind live data (the score ring's halo) and marketing surfaces. Never on a panel, never on text a reader is parsing.
- Arrivals are staggered, not simultaneous: header pills, money rows and heat cells land oldest-first with delays under 300ms total. A grid caps its cascade so scale never slows arrival.
- Count-ups run on `setInterval`. The real value is the default render — if the tween never starts, the number on screen must still be correct.

## Copy rules, enforced in review

- Descriptive, never prescriptive. No "you should", no "consider", no urgency.
- No exclamation marks. No coaching tone. Sentence case everywhere.
- Buttons name what happens ("Recompute score", never "Submit"). Card headings say something rather than labelling.
- Empty states name the next move, never "nothing here yet".
- Banned: supercharge, unleash, 10x, revolutionize, game-changing, seamless, effortless, "AI-powered" as a headline, growth hacking, "in seconds".
- Numbers describe behaviour — never a benchmark and never another person — except in explicit percentile surfaces, which are `--signal`.
- One notification a day. Never a price alert.

## Code conventions

- Next.js App Router. Server components by default; `'use client'` only where interaction requires it.
- `lib/score`, `lib/engine` and `lib/cards` are pure functions — no I/O.
- **One paid plan, and it only sells what a server route actually checks.** Gating lives in `lib/tiers.ts` and nowhere else: components ask `can(user, capability)`, and no component reads `user.tier` directly. There were two tiers and twelve capabilities, six of which had no implementation at all — a paywall whose feature list is longer than its feature set is the one thing a paywall must never be. `Tier` is `free | pro`; `Capability` is the five the routes check (`reportCarousel`, `correlationCard`, `publicationExport`, `transparentExport`, `liveBadge`). `Capability` has no member for minting, sharing or rarity, so those gates cannot be written — sharing is never paywalled, and `canMintCards()`/`canShareCards()` take no tier on purpose. **Every read of a stored tier goes through `normaliseTier()`**, which maps the older `plus`/`trader` names forward: a rename in this repo must not drop a paying subscriber to free. Stripe is the source of truth; `tierFromStatus()` downgrades to free unless the status is live. There is one tier module — `lib/billing/tiers.ts` was a second, dead but tested, disagreeing with the live one on both members and signature, and it is gone.
- **What Pro buys has a surface.** The four export capabilities were enforced in API routes nothing in the app linked to, so the plan was a feature list with no button. They are a hairline-ruled block at the foot of `/you`, live on Pro and stating the plan name when not — no blurred figure, because a plausible number under a blur is the one thing this product must not print.
- **`/pricing` states the plans off `FREE_ALWAYS` and `CAPABILITY_LABEL`** rather than a hand-written list, so the page cannot drift from what the gates enforce, and starts a checkout in place for a signed-in free account. It lives in `(marketing)`, wears the shared `Chrome` nav and footer, and speaks `--mk-*`. Every lock links there; nothing opens a modal.
- **The trial is seven days from the brokerage connecting**, not from account creation — an account with no ledger has nothing to unlock and would burn the window on an empty screen. It states an end date once, where the plan is described. No countdown, no nag.
- Monetization is present, never blocking. A locked tile occupies the slot its unlocked twin would, blurred, with the tier named — never a modal wall in front of a screen the user asked for. Every lock carries **readiness computed from real sample counts** (`Ready now` / `38 tags to go`); never fake a Ready. No countdown, no discount banner, no trial nag.
- **A locked card's share button is absent, not disabled.** Never show a user an affordance that then refuses them.
- `<ShareButton>` is the one share affordance and it mints through `/api/cards/mint`: the client names a kind and never supplies contents, so a crafted request cannot mint a card claiming a number that never happened. What comes back is a 96-bit slug, which is the card's whole access model. `/card/[type]/[id]` is the typed URL and 301s to `/c/[slug]`, where the OpenGraph tags live — two dynamic names cannot share a path segment, which is why there is no `/og/[type]/[id]`.
- Share cards are public by slug: `cardBySlug` takes no userId and projects only what a card renders. The slug is 96 bits of randomness, because it is the card's entire access model.
- Two model providers, with a hard split: **Anthropic writes every sentence** in the product (the daily insight), **OpenAI only draws** — the Wrapped backdrop and the sixteen archetype avatars. No generated prose comes from OpenAI and no generated imagery comes from Anthropic.
- The sixteen archetypes in `lib/archetypes.ts` are the corners of a four-bit cube — each score component is above the fixed bar (60) or it is not — so nothing is curated to reach a round number and every profile lands in exactly one. The bar is fixed rather than relative to the reader's own mean, because an archetype goes on a share card and has to mean the same thing whoever wears it. No model is involved.
- **Archetype avatars are sixteen cartoon characters, drawn by us in `lib/avatars/drawn.ts`, and that file is what ships.** An archetype is the one thing in the product that goes beside a person's name, and the two earlier passes — thin line marks, then neon glyphs from an image model — both read as instrumentation. Each character is built out of the abstract form named in `ARCHETYPES[n].emblem`, so the table stays the single description of what an archetype looks like, and moss still carries the reading: more moss in a character is more components above the bar. The face is two punched dots and a mouth — a rendered human face beside someone's name would be a claim about who they are. `public/archetypes` is empty and `lib/avatars/manifest.ts` is generated from it (`npm run avatars:manifest`), never hand-edited; a PNG dropped there by `npm run avatars` overrides the drawn character for that key, so `lib/avatars/prompt.ts` asks for the same flat cartoon mascot and never names the archetype — a model given the word "Sentinel" draws a guard.
- **Card art is thirteen fixed images, one per kind**, authored once by `npm run backdrops` and committed to `public/cards`. Nothing generates at request time and nothing is stored per card. This is the Wrapped model: a template is designed once and worn by millions, and what makes a card someone's own is composited over it in type — their figure, their sentence, their company. Minting is instant and free.
- `lib/wrapped/brief.ts` and `scenes.ts` are pure and exist to *author* those thirteen, not to run per user: four measured quantities (magnitude, direction, texture, density) taken from a representative ledger shape each scene, so the picture for a kind matches what that kind is about.
- **In-app card artwork is drawn, and a photograph is the ground under it.** `components/cards/Artwork.tsx` holds twelve flat hard-edged figures, one per kind, and behind each sits one Unsplash photograph — desaturated, luminosity-blended and washed to the card's hue, so it reads as material rather than as a subject. Both halves are finished on their own: a kind with no photograph wears the drawn geometry alone, which is the design and not a gap. The queries in `lib/unsplash/query.ts` search for texture and never for a person, a desk or a laptop — a photograph of someone's life makes a claim about the reader the ledger never made.
- **Two cards wear the photograph across the whole face, and the list is `FULL_GROUND` in `WrappedCard.tsx`: `wrapped` and `archetype`.** They are the identity cards — the year, and who the year says you are — and neither carries a figure or a chart that a busy ground would make harder to read. A card on a full ground paints no band backing and no second copy of the picture (`<Artwork onGround>`), because two exposures of one photograph at two opacities is a seam, not a composition. Everywhere else the band stays a band.
- Photographs come from Unsplash through `lib/unsplash`, split the way the icon layer is: `query.ts` is pure and tested (queries, referral parameters, credit, CDN sizing), `fetch.ts` is the only caller. **The store holds the URL, never the bytes** — their terms require hotlinking from their CDN — plus the credit that has to travel with it. Filling it is deliberate and manual: `npm run photos`, or `POST /api/photos/refresh` behind `PHOTOS_REFRESH_TOKEN`. **A page only ever reads the store**, so no amount of traffic can spend quota, and re-running the fill re-picks every photograph under artefacts people have already posted. `/legal/photos` names each photographer and links both them and Unsplash with the referral parameters their guidelines require; like `/legal/icons` it reads the store rather than calling the API.
- **The company is the per-person half.** A card about a position wears that instrument's mark and ticker through `<Logo>`, and the Wrapped card leads with the three names you closed the most round trips in — ranked by count, never by P&L, because ranking a retrospective by profit turns it into a leaderboard.
- The model never draws a glyph. Every figure on a card is set in type by us; a generated number is one nobody can correct, on an artefact whose whole claim is that its numbers came from a brokerage.
- The art prompt (`lib/wrapped/prompt.ts`) is pure and tested. It forbids text, numerals, logos, people and objects, and asks for a quiet centre — the card's own type is set in Outfit by us, and a model drawing a number would be both wrong and unfixable.
- Company marks come from logo.dev through `/api/logo/[symbol]`, never a direct URL: the token stays server-side and the route always answers with an image, so no component handles a broken one. Unknown tickers get a drawn monogram in the palette.
- Icons come from The Noun Project through `/api/icon/[name]`, signed with two-legged OAuth 1.0a in `lib/icons/noun.ts` (pure and tested; the secret never leaves the server). Fetched once and stored in Mongo with no TTL — an icon does not change, every call costs quota, and their terms forbid redistribution, so the route must never accept a search term. Third-party SVG is sanitized before it is stored. Icons are CC-BY: `/legal/icons` names the creators, and it reads the store rather than calling the API.
- Market data (Finnhub, `lib/market`) backchecks the brokerage and never becomes an event. A quote may only correct a mark the last sync left stale — never replace a mark from today, and never surface a move on its own. There is no price alert and no place to write one. `resolveMark` also refuses a quote that disagrees with the broker by more than half: that is a symbol resolving to a different instrument, not a correction.
- Anything a screen shows from market data says so, in mono, via `provenanceLine`. The brokerage stays the account of record: names fill a gap the broker left empty and never overwrite one it gave.
- `lib/market/marks.ts` and `holdings.ts` are pure and tested; `client.ts` owns the network. Calls go through `cached()` — a shared Mongo collection swept by a TTL index, keyed by request and not by user, because the rate limit is per key. Fan-out is bounded by `pooled()`. Every failure path returns the brokerage's own numbers rather than an error.
- `app/og/[slug]/render.tsx`, `lib/logos/fetch.ts`, `lib/email/render.ts`, `lib/wrapped/prompt.ts` and `lib/avatars/prompt.ts` are the files allowed to repeat palette hexes, and the reason is the same in all five: an image at a URL and a message in an inbox both carry no stylesheet, and a prompt describing a colour to a model cannot cite a custom property. Keep the values in step with the `--share-*` tokens, and give every multi-child element an explicit `display`. Their tests (`prompt.test.ts`) necessarily repeat the values they assert. `app/api/badge/[slug]/route.ts` is in the same class — a self-contained SVG at a URL carries no stylesheet. So is `themeColor` in `app/layout.tsx`: a browser chrome colour is a meta tag, and a meta tag cannot cite a custom property, so it is the one place `--bg`'s value is written twice and the two have to be kept in step by hand. `components/cards/StoryViewer.module.css` is the one component allowed `#000`/`#fff`: a fullscreen story player is a lightbox, and a lightbox is black with white chrome whatever the theme does. The landing is `app/(marketing)/page.tsx` + `landing.module.css` plus `BagMark`, `WrappedDeck`, `FirstWeek`, `PnlChart` and `WaitlistForm`: a white hero — two phone mocks apart, the equity line curling across the gap to the Wrapped handset, annotation bubbles on their edges — over a dark field carrying the Wrapped deck, the coming-soon score, the behaviour reads, the first week and the waitlist. Nothing there escapes the `--mk-*` tokens. Every animated piece is a client component whose **static state is the finished design**: the deck renders the full pile with card one on top, `FirstWeek` renders step one selected, and both refuse to start their timer under `prefers-reduced-motion`. Any manual move — drag, arrow, dot, step — stops the autoplay for good rather than fighting the reader for control. The waitlist posts to `/api/waitlist` (idempotent upsert by lowercased email; the client never writes anything but an email and a tier name). **There is no invented social proof and there must not be one again** — "Loved by 320K investors · 4.9 rating" over three drawn faces, and "12,408 investors, same 45 days", were on the page on the launch day of a product with no users; a fabricated review count is a bad idea on any landing and a liability on a financial one. The hero pill keeps the stacked-circle treatment and fills it with broker marks instead of faces — "Robinhood, Fidelity, Schwab and 20+ more", the coverage `/start` already states rather than a new claim invented for the hero, and the actual first question a visitor has. The sample ledger behind `PnlChart` and the phone mocks is illustrative and says so in its own tail. The nav and footer are `Chrome.tsx`, shared with `/pricing`, and the landing is `force-dynamic` because a statically prerendered page freezes its `APP_UNLOCKED` read at build time.
- Engine findings are descriptive and self-silencing: below their sample floor they return `null` rather than report a coincidence, and the evidence line must never contradict the sentence it sits under. A finding's `impact` is the realised P&L of the pattern's own bucket, summed off the round trips — never a projection, never an extrapolation; where no honest bucket exists (a ratio, a sizing multiple) it stays `null` and the screen shows no figure. Ledger-only findings are materialised in `derived.findings` per sync; tag-joined ones are computed live on Patterns because tags move without a sync.
- Screens are assembled from `components/primitives`, `components/idioms`, `components/app` and `components/cards`; new visual patterns start there, not inline.
- The tag loop (`lib/tags.ts`, `<TagPrompt>`) is the only input a brokerage cannot supply and every correlation is downstream of it, which is why it sits second on Home rather than in settings. Two taps, no text field — a free-text reason cannot be grouped, and a reason that cannot be grouped cannot become a finding. `TagDoc` persists the reason lower-cased, so go through `whyKey()`/`whyLabel()` rather than comparing strings.
- Screen derivations live in `app/(app)/derive.ts` and are pure. Nothing there invents a figure: where the ledger cannot answer, the function returns an empty result rather than a plausible one.

## Operations

- **`.env.example` is the contract**, and every integration degrades gracefully when its variables are missing. That is load-bearing rather than polite: CI builds with no secrets at all, so a build that needed a key to compile would be a regression.
- **Two gates, deliberately not one flag.** `APP_UNLOCKED=1` opens the product; `DEBUG_PAGE=1` opens `/debug`, which names every secret, prints the raw ledger and carries live Sync and Score buttons. They were the same flag, which meant launching the product published that page.
- **The nightly jobs sweep, they do not loop.** Both used to iterate every connected user serially inside one request under a 60-second cap — about two accounts before the platform killed it, and everyone behind the cut silently unscored. `lib/db/cron.ts` gives them a stored cursor: each call takes the next slice, stops *starting* users before its budget runs out, and advances past a user who threw rather than parking the table behind them. `closesSweep()` is pure and tested, because wrapping early skips people and never wrapping stops the job while it still answers 200. The schedule is a Railway cron service running `npm run cron`; `vercel.json` declared crons on a platform this app does not deploy to and never fired once.
- **Export and erasure are buttons on `/profile`, not a support address.** Both are defined off one list in `lib/db/account.ts`, and a test asserts every collection in the store is either erased or explicitly exempt with a stated reason — a delete that misses a collection while the export still shows it is a promise broken silently. Deletion cancels a live Stripe subscription first: emptying our mirror would leave one billing that nobody in the product can see.
- **`/legal/privacy` and `/legal/terms` are written against the code**, not from a template. Every claim in them is checkable in this repository — including that the fact pack sent to Anthropic carries no name, no email, no ticker and no dollar figure. If that stops being true, the page changes in the same commit. `SUPPORT_EMAIL` is the address they print.
- **`metadataBase` is required, not decorative.** Without it the `opengraph-image` convention emits a relative path no unfurler can fetch, which on a product whose entire pitch is that people post it is a broken feature. `lib/site.ts` resolves the origin the same way `lib/origin.ts` does — `APP_URL` first.
- **`npm run typecheck`, `npm run lint` and `npm test` all pass, and CI runs them plus the build.** ESLint's config turns off `no-img-element` on purpose: every `<img>` here points at a URL whose bytes Next must not touch, and Unsplash's terms *require* hotlinking from their CDN, so `next/image`'s proxy would be a licence violation rather than an optimisation.
