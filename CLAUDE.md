# Supercruise

A flight recorder for your portfolio. Read-only, permanently: Supercruise writes down what the account did — it never places a trade, never recommends one, never alerts on price. The instrument records; it does not fly the aircraft, and that is the whole product.

**The brand is the lowercase wordmark `supercruise` and the dart mark** — a swept dart crossing a broken ring, drawn once as `SupercruiseMark` in `components/brand/SupercruiseMark.tsx` and mirrored in `app/icon.svg`. Everything in it is `currentColor` and nothing is knocked out, which is the change from the level mark it replaces: that one punched its counter through a filled disc and needed a `ground` prop to say what colour the counter should read as. A stroked ring with a solid dart across it needs no counter, so one drawing sits on the black field, on a white marketing hero and on a share card with no variant and no prop. **The ring is broken where the dart crosses it**, at the two points the flight path enters and leaves, and that break is the idea: the instrument does not contain the thing it measures — the thing is going through it and out the far side. `pathLength` normalises the circumference to 100 so the gaps are percentage points; the dash list must have an **even** number of values, because SVG repeats an odd list to make it even and lays a stray tick on the second lap. Supercruise is sustained supersonic flight without afterburner — speed you hold rather than speed you spend, which is the same claim this product makes about an account: not how fast you went once, but whether you can hold that altitude. No pilot and no hand is drawn: a figure beside somebody's own ledger is a claim about them the ledger never made. The wordmark is never capitalised in UI chrome; prose says Supercruise.

**The aesthetic is flight: rockets, jets, the stratosphere, sustained speed.** It replaces an instrument-and-ledger direction (a spirit level, steady hands, fitness tracking) that described the same product in a quieter register. What carries over unchanged is the *discipline* — the black field, the rationed colour, the honest-figures rule, absent-rather-than-defaulted — because none of that was ever about the metaphor. What changes is the vocabulary, and it changes **only where a flight word says the thing more clearly than the word it replaces**: the tagline is *a flight recorder for your portfolio*, which is the sharpest statement of the product's own hardest constraint — the instrument writes down what the aircraft did and never touches the stick. **A metaphor that makes a reading less clear is a bad trade and does not ship.** The sixteen archetypes keep their names, because they are stored in Mongo by key and go beside a person's name; the panel eyebrows keep saying what they measure; no figure, label or provenance line is reworded to sound like a cockpit. Aviation reaches the brand, the lede, the waiting states, the grounds and the artwork, and stops at the instruments — with one exception, the score dial, which earns it because an attitude indicator says something the arc alone cannot.

**The app is black, and the colour on it is rationed to meaning.** It was light-with-a-dark-mode, then midnight plum with six hue families over it, then black and white with no hue at all — and the last of those was one step too far: a screen where money, the score and a part-to-whole chart are all steps of grey reads washed out, and two greys are the distinction a reader is least able to make. The ground stays `#000000` with near-black plates (`--s1` `#0C0C0C`), and the six families are saturated again *on that ground*, which is where they can afford to be brighter than the plum field ever allowed. `app/layout.tsx` stamps `data-mode="dark"` permanently and `themeColor` is `#000000`, the one place the ground's value is written twice and the two have to be kept in step by hand. Marketing is unaffected: it speaks `--mk-*`, stated once and never flipped by mode. **What the monochrome pass left behind is the part worth keeping: every meaning is now carried by form as well as by hue.** Direction is a fill — solid gained, hatched lost — *and* green versus red; rank is a numeral in a chip *and* a step down the ramp; the allocation ring identifies by colour *and* by order, with every share stated in type. Nothing is conveyed by colour alone, so nothing fails for a reader who cannot separate two hues — and nobody has to read a chart in greys to get that.

**The product is Supercruise and so is everything in this repository — for the first time in three renames.** It was Canopy, then Bagcheck, then Steadyhands. Each of those renames reached the wordmark, the mark, every sentence and the icon, and each deliberately stopped at storage, on the reasoning that a database name is an address rather than a brand. The names have now been carried the rest of the way: the `MONGODB_DB` fallback in `lib/db/client.ts`, `scripts/seed.mjs`, the two harness scripts, the npm `name`, the lockfile and the font licence all say supercruise. **What makes that safe is one fact, and it is a fact rather than a hope: `MONGODB_DB` is set explicitly on the deployment**, so the live ledger sits in whatever database that variable names and the fallback is never consulted there. The risk the old comment warned about has not gone away, it has moved — **if `MONGODB_DB` is ever unset on a deployment holding real data, that line now points it at a fresh, empty database and every live account reads as new.** The four places that name the database move together or not at all. **Three addresses are outside this repository and still say bagcheck**: the GitHub repository, the Railway service and the `*.up.railway.app` host it generated. Those are real addresses with live traffic and inbound links; changing them is a deployment action, not an edit. **The one thing a rename must reach and keeps missing is the unfurl.** `app/opengraph-image.tsx` was still drawing the *bag* mark two brands later, and `app/icon.svg` was still the canopy dome one brand later: an unfurl and a favicon are the only surfaces nobody looks at from inside the product, which is exactly how a logo goes three renames stale without anyone noticing. Both are drawn by hand from `SupercruiseMark`'s own geometry and have to change in the same commit as it.

**Five app pages, on one nav.** `/you` is the dashboard, `/holdings` the position table, `/insights` the patterns, `/trophies` the case, `/wrapped` the year. `components/app/AppNav.tsx` is the whole of the navigation: the mark, the tabs, the sync pill and the avatar (the door to `/profile`, which is not a tab). The active tab is a 2px rule in full ink that is always drawn and only changes value, so nothing shifts as you move — it was amber, and a semantic family borrowed to paint chrome is the one thing this palette does not allow. There is no rail and no mobile tab bar — the nav scrolls horizontally below 1080px.

**The dashboard is built from `components/dash`.** `Chrome.tsx` holds the page frame, panel, stat card and chips; `Race.tsx` the five-lane race; `Charts.tsx` the P&L columns, allocation donut, weekday bars, hold meters and sparkline; `Cards.tsx` the Wrapped promo, insight rows and the locked tile. Four pages share one panel rather than four near-copies that drift a pixel at a time.

**The Race is the set piece, and every lane is a fund with a ticker.** All five cars run for the same 2.6 seconds, so a longer lane can only read as a faster car. There is no hedge-fund index in this repository and no key for one, so nothing is labelled "hedge funds" in the aggregate: the field is four funds that exist to replicate hedge-fund strategies (`DBMF`, `QAI`, `MNA`, `BTAL`) plus the S&P, each quoted through `indexReturnYtd` on a six-hour cache keyed by symbol rather than by reader. A fund the provider will not quote is dropped, never drawn at zero; under two rows the block is absent; and it is absent again unless the reader's own curve reaches the first fortnight of January. The leader's car body is a real `<linearGradient>` — SVG has no CSS-gradient paint value, and a browser handed one drops the attribute and paints the car black, which is what the mock did.

**The Wrapped deck is chaotic on purpose, and each of the twelve owns its art direction.** It shipped on one shared `STYLE_PREFIX` — "near-black deep canvas, luminous grainy gradients" — which is why twelve cards came back looking like one card printed twelve times. Every card now carries `art: { medium, texture, palette }` in `wrapped/cards.mjs`: chrome, risograph, cut paper, gradient mesh, halftone, thermal, CRT, marbled ink, terrazzo, blueprint, airbrush, foil. Four of them are **light** cards, which is what stops the deck reading as one dark poster. `promptFor` leads with the medium, because an image model weights the front of a prompt hardest. What stays shared is only `CONSTRAINTS` — every figure is still set in type by us, so a prompt that let a model draw a numeral would be a number nobody can correct. `COMPOSITION` is not shared either: it is stated once per **placement**, and it is the negative of wherever that card's lockup sits, because asking for a quiet lower third on a card whose type is at the head points the model's incident straight through the type. **The variation is across the twelve and not across readers**: card five is the same direction for everybody, the way a Wrapped template is.

**The type varies with the art, along four axes, and the machine line does not vary at all.** Twelve grounds under twelve identical lockups is a deck of one poster in twelve wallpapers, so each card also carries `type: { face, place, align, case, order }` in `wrapped/cards.mjs` — seven display voices (`machine`, `voice`, `poster`, `serif`, `grotesk`, `geometric`, `lede`), four placements on the 1080×1920 stage (`foot`, `head`, `middle`, `split`), three edges to set from, and three orders for the lockup's own parts. **No two of the twelve share a face, a placement and an edge**, and the vocabulary is closed — a value with no rule in `card.css`, or a rule no card asks for, fails a test. `card.css` states rules per *axis* rather than per card, so twelve posters cost about fifteen rules. **The type gets a ground of its own and the art runs clean above it.** Each placement sets `--band`, the pixel row where the reading zone starts, and both the scrim and the lockup's padding read that one number so they cannot drift apart — a scrim pinned to the foot while the type sits at the head dims the art where nothing is read and leaves the type over whatever the model drew. **In pixels, never percent**: percentage padding resolves against the containing block's *width* in both axes, so `--band: 66.7%` fed to `padding-top` put the lockup at 38% of the height and stranded it above its own scrim. The bands are **measured, not chosen** — a lockup is an eyebrow, a hero label, a 380px hero, a title, a rule and two facts, about 900px, which is half the stage rather than the third it looks like it should be; reserving a literal third pushed the type onto raw art, which is worse than reserving nothing. The rule of thirds here is the compositional one, not the arithmetic one. `npm run wrapped:check -- --render` asserts the lockup lands inside its band, and `BANDS` there is the same table `card.css` scrims — two copies of one table, so if either moves the other moves in the same commit. **The eyebrow, the fact labels and the fact values stay in `Machine` on all twelve**: mono owns labels, counts and timestamps here, and a deck whose *labels* also moved would be chaos in the one layer that has to stay scannable. **Every default is stated on `.card` itself, not only on `[data-face]`** — a card minted before this table carries none of these attributes, and an unresolved `var()` inside `font-family` invalidates the declaration rather than falling through, so the defaults are what keep an old stored card drawing the way it drew.

**`wrapped/templates/*.html` are generated and committed, and a rename has to regenerate them.** They still said `bagcheck` two brands later, and the card footer still wore the bag era's amber dot — the same class of miss as the unfurl and the favicon, for the same reason: a card footer is a surface nobody looks at from inside the product. `npm run wrapped:templates` rebuilds them from `scripts/build-wrapped-templates.mjs`, and that has to run in the same commit as any change to the wordmark, the mark or the eyebrow. The footer mark is the dart in `currentColor`, so it reads on the four light cards and the eight dark ones with no variant.

**A card is finished before its art exists.** The twelve palettes live in `tokens.css` as `--wrap-NN-*` and `card.css` draws each ground in CSS under `.bg`. When the generated image lands it covers that; when it does not — a fresh deployment, a missing key — the card is still the card. **The art set is versioned (`ART_SET` in the template builder, `public/wrapped/2026/art/<set>`) and never overwritten**, because an image drawn to one direction sitting under a card designed as another is worse than no image at all. The set is `stratosphere-01`, and it is drawn. `chaotic-01` was declared and never drawn, so every card rendered on its CSS ground for as long as that was true — which is the design working, not a gap. **The reason it was never drawn is that `scripts/generate-backgrounds.mjs` had been failing at import**: it went on importing `STYLE_PREFIX` from `wrapped/cards.mjs` after the deck moved to a per-card medium, texture and palette, so `npm run wrapped:backgrounds` died before it reached the API. It calls `drawBackground`/`promptFor` from `lib/wrapped/backgrounds.ts` now, which is the drift that file's own note predicted — one prompt, one place. `--print` dumps the twelve prompts and spends nothing, because twelve high-quality portraits is real money and the prompt is the artefact. **The twelve media did not move with the direction**: chrome, riso, cut paper, mesh, halftone, thermal, CRT, marbled ink, terrazzo, blueprint, airbrush, foil is what stopped the deck reading as one card printed twelve times, and pointing all twelve at a jet would rebuild that failure exactly. What moved is the **subject** — a riso contrail, a blueprint wing section, a thermal exhaust plume, marbled storm cloud, halftone shock diamonds — atmospheric abstraction rather than aircraft portraits.

**Where the ledger cannot answer, the slot is drawn and the condition is stated.** Peer percentiles, "top 3% of supercruise", view counts and friends-beaten need a population this product does not have. They are `<LockedCard>`s: the slot its unlocked twin would occupy, a drawing carrying **no numerals**, the one condition that would fill it, and a button to be told when it lands. A card carrying a plausible number is worse than a card carrying none, on the screen whose whole claim is that its figures came off a brokerage.

**`lib/dash.ts` is the dashboard's arithmetic, and it is pure.** Realised P&L there comes off `derived.roundTrips[].pnl`, never `derived.dailyPnl[].realised` — that series is the *cash amount* of every sell and dividend, so a reader who sold half a million dollars of stock for a two-thousand-dollar profit would read "+$500,000". Sharpe is annualised off the equity curve's daily marks with a zero risk-free rate and the screen says so; it is null under sixty marks. Win rate is the ratio of profitable round trips to all of them, off the FIFO matches — arithmetic, never a model — and it is null under ten round trips, because a rate over three is a coin landing heads twice. **Below the floor the card says so** rather than quietly becoming a different statistic under the same heading: the figure falls back to positions in profit and the tail names the win rate and how many trips are still needed. `holdSplit` reports which *direction* the asymmetry runs, because a screen that only knows how to phrase "you cut winners faster" prints a sentence contradicting its own chart when the reader does the opposite.

**The equity curve is the account when the brokerage says, and the book when it will not.** The sync reads `getUserAccountBalance` per account and stores `cash` on the snapshot; `equityFrom` adds it and — because a money-market fund is reported *twice*, once in `cash` and again as a position flagged `cash_equivalent` — drops the equivalents from the positions side when cash is known. Every mark carries `withCash`, and a window is on the account basis only when **every** mark in it agrees: a curve that started carrying cash in June would otherwise step on that day and read the balance as a gain. The basis picks the flows too, and this is the part worth remembering — on a **book** curve the thing that moves it other than the market is money crossing into a position, so `investmentFlows` (buys and sells) comes out; on an **account** curve a buy is an internal transfer that moves nothing, so `transferFlows` (deposits and withdrawals) comes out. Netting the wrong one reports a deposit left in cash as a total loss. The screen states which basis it is on, in the stat tail and in the provenance strip.

**A hue means one thing, and there is no longer an exception.** `--wedge-1`–`--wedge-5` existed so a pie could identify its slices, which is the one thing a palette of five families with one meaning each cannot do — they were the single written exemption in `tokens.css`. The ring is gone and the treemap replaced it: area says how much, order says which, and the fill means money again. The tokens are withdrawn rather than left lying about for the next chart to reach for. `--moss` money up, `--loss` money down, `--signal` comparison and exposure, `--ember` scarcity and the live, `--accent` the product's own voice — and `--w1`–`--w5`, ordered steps down `--signal`, for a part-to-whole *bar*.

**Small text on the dark field needs 60% white, not the 28–42% the mock used.** The design's dim ramp fails WCAG 1.4.3 across the board — 197 failures on the first sweep. `--meta` is 58%, `--nav-dim` 62%, `--ink3` 66%, and any `color: rgba(255,255,255,…)` under 60% is a bug. Backgrounds, borders and tracks are meant to be faint and are exempt.

## Colour

- `styles/tokens.css` is the single source of colour. A palette hex anywhere else in `app/`, `components/`, `lib/`, or `styles/` is a bug. Always `var(--*)`; when a value needs alpha, `color-mix(in srgb, var(--token) N%, transparent)`. (`docs/` is imported reference material and exempt.)
- **Black ground, saturated families on it.** The app was light-first with a dark mode, then dark plum, then black with a single ramp of white. It is `#000000` with the six families back — brighter than the plum field allowed, because pure black gives a hue room. `:root` still holds the full light palette and `html[data-mode="dark"]` still overrides it; `app/layout.tsx` ships `data-mode="dark"` and `themeColor: "#000000"`, and nothing downstream ever learns which mode it is in — a rule that names a mode is a rule in the wrong file. **The light block is untouched and unselected**: it is history, not a second product, and `<ModeSwitch>` is not on any surface.
- Canvas `--bg` (#000000), plates `--s1` (#0C0C0C) and `--tile` (#111111), chip fill `--inset`. **A panel is a near-black plate on black, held by a 1px `--edge` and the softest possible lift** (`--lift`; `--lift-hero` under the hero). Rows inside a list divide with `--line-light`; controls outline with `--line2`. No heavy borders, no glass, no bevel — and the plate is never pure black, or it has nothing to sit on.
- **Six families, each with one job**, and each stated twice — a light value and a dark one, because a hue legible on black is unreadable on white:
  - `--moss` (dark `#35E07F`) — **money up, and nothing else.**
  - `--loss` (dark `#FF5A70`) — money down, and nothing else. `--loss-deep` `#FF2D4A` is the single worst column in a chart.
  - `--signal` (dark `#A4B0BD`, cool steel) — exposure, comparison, percentile. **This is the one family meant to sit back**, and it is what every lane in the race that is not the reader's own takes.
  - `--ember` (dark `#F59E0B`) — streaks, records, scarcity and the live.
  - `--accent` (dark `#A78BFA`) — the product's own voice: the written daily insight, Wrapped identity, Investor Age.
  - `--gold` (`#FFC857`) — the score, and only the score: the hero numeral, its ring, the four component arcs, and the `--h1`–`--h4` ramp the year of days is drawn in.
- **Form carries every meaning a hue carries, in parallel — that is what the monochrome pass bought and it is not to be given back:**
  - **Direction is fill as well as colour.** A gained bar is solid; a lost bar is hatched behind a 1px hairline — in the P&L columns, in the race, and in the legend that names them. A direction stated in colour alone is one a large share of readers never see.
  - **Rank is a numeral.** The race draws each row's standing in a hairline chip, and the reader's is the one inverted chip on the surface.
  - **"Which" is colour *and* order.** The allocation ring takes `--wedge-1`–`--wedge-5` plus `--wedge-rest`, fixed and ordered by weight, so the largest holding is always violet and the remainder always neutral — and every share is in type in the legend, which is the accessible chart.
- **The archetype character keeps its family colour, in the app and on a share card alike.** It is the one fully saturated drawing in the product and the one thing that goes beside a person's name, so it is where colour is worth spending. A monochrome pass took it to ink for a while, and a grey character on a black screen reads as instrumentation — which is the exact failure the two earlier avatar passes were rejected for.
- **A `color-mix` used as text has to clear 4.5:1 at the *mixed* value, not at the token.** `--loss` at `#8A8A8A` passed on its own while its 82% mix on the holdings table read 4.14:1. The sweep caught it; nothing else would have.
- **The light values are set by contrast, not by taste**, and so are the dark ones. On black the floor is easier and the failures move to the mixes: any `color: rgba(255,255,255,…)` under 60% is a bug, and any `color-mix` of a family used as *text* has to be checked at the mixed value. Backgrounds, borders and tracks are meant to be faint and are exempt.
- **A hue never fills a surface, never paints chrome, and never marks a hover.** It reaches a figure, a 3px meter, a 1px semantic edge, and stops. If a screen looks colourful, something took a colour it did not earn. The rail's active tab is `--ink-fill`, not a hue — navigation furniture is the clearest case of chrome there is.
- **The score component meters are `signal`, all four.** They were moss, moss, moss, signal; the figure beside each is what carries the reading, and four saturated bars in a hero plus four more below it was the largest single block of colour in the product.
- **A density grid is a texture, not a fill.** `HeatGrid` caps its cells at 15px and scrolls past its panel rather than stretching them: a hundred-odd cells at full panel width become 24px tiles and the panel reads as one saturated block, which is the same failure whatever hue it is in.
- **Glow is rationed to live data, and in light it is not a glow at all.** `--glow-*` and `--halo-*` mark a key metric that is measured and current — the score ring, the sync dot, an active streak. On the dark field they are light; on white the same tokens resolve to a 1px ring and a wash at 5% that you notice only by its absence. One halo per surface (`.hero` takes `data-halo`). Never chrome, never a heading, never a hover state.
- The strongest object in a view is the primary action: `--ink-field` — near-black on white, white on dark. The inversion is the strongest gesture available either way, so it is spent on exactly one thing per screen.
- **The field behind the shell is the stratosphere, and it is dark-only.** It was a ridge of light, which belonged to a brand about being level; it is now the curve of a horizon low in the frame, the band of atmosphere on it, two offset star fields and one contrail on the mark's own 124° axis — all gradients, no image, nothing to fail. Two star fields rather than one, because a single repeating dot grid reads as a screen door and the beat between two pitches is what makes it read as a scatter. The suppression is unchanged and is the point: 0.3 opacity, behind the canvas, drifting over 62s. `app/(app)/app.module.css` hides `.field` outside dark mode — at any strength that reads as texture on black it reads as a smudge on white.
- Status is never encoded by colour alone: every state carries a word. Five hues with one meaning each only works if the meaning is also written down.
- Share cards keep the `--share-*` and `--card-*` families and their hue art, declared once on `:root` and **never flipped by mode** — a card is an artefact with its own palette, saturated well past anything the app uses, and an artefact that changed colour with the reader's preference would not be one artefact. That gap is the editorial contrast rather than a leak.
- **There is no waitlist, and there must not be one again.** A form collecting addresses against a feature with no date is a promise this repository cannot keep, and it was on the nav, the hero, the score section and the plans section — four asks for an email on a page selling a product that ships today. `WaitlistForm` and `/api/waitlist` are gone; the `waitlist` collection stays declared and stays in `PERSONAL_COLLECTIONS` so erasure still covers anything written while it was live, exactly like `pulses`.
- **One plan, lying down.** A tier card is a column because it is meant to sit beside two other columns and be compared with them; with nothing to compare it to, the same shape is a narrow strip of text in the middle of a very wide screen. `#plans` is landscape now — the price and the door on the left, what you get in two columns beside it.
- **The tagline is "a fitness tracker for your portfolio".** It was "a flight recorder for your portfolio", which is the sharper sentence about the product's hardest constraint and the vaguer one about what it does for a stranger. The shape is the Whoop comparison without the brand in it: the claim is ours to make, and a real company's name on a financial marketing page is a liability the sentence does not need.
- **The hero handsets are photographs of the app.** They were drawn in JSX — an illustration kept in step with the product by hand, which nobody does — so the phone showed an account balance and a holdings list for months after `/you` started leading with the score. `npm run marketing:shots` captures `/you` and `/wrapped?demo=1` at handset width against the same seeded example ledger the demo deck serves, and writes two PNGs into `public/marketing`. Committed, because a landing page must not depend on a screenshot service; deliberate, because a capture on every build would rewrite two binaries in every commit. **The ledger behind them is the example one** — a real person's positions on a public marketing page is the one thing a screenshot here must never be.
- **The marketing nav is a floating pill, and it is solid at every scroll position.** It was a full-bleed row of links at the top of the document, scrolling away with it — which put the one door into the product out of reach for the whole of a very long page. It is a sticky capsule now: dark ground, white ink, a hairline that keeps it an object over the dark field where the capsule and the ground are within a few points of each other. The obvious version of this fades a transparent bar into a solid one on leaving the hero, which means inverting the ink mid-scroll, and the static state of *that* — what a reader without scroll timelines gets — is whichever half of the flip it was authored in. One ground and one ink needs nothing to be true. `.page` had to move from `overflow-x: hidden` to `overflow-x: clip`: hidden on one axis computes the other to `auto`, which makes the element a scroll container, and a sticky child anchors to the nearest scrollport — so the pill would have anchored to a box that never scrolls and simply never stuck.
- **Sections expand as you reach them, and the expand exists only where scroll timelines do.** `[data-expand]` is declared entirely inside the `@supports`/`prefers-reduced-motion` block rather than as a base rule that gets retargeted, because an expand has no sensible timed fallback — a browser without scroll timelines and a reader who asked for less motion both get the finished band with nothing to wait for. It is a `clip-path: inset()` opening rather than a `scale`, because a band that scales up scales its own type and every line inside is soft for the length of the tween; both ends are `inset()` with a `round`, or the two shapes will not interpolate.
- **The hero handset leads with the read, because the dashboard does.** The phone showed an account value and a holdings list — the screen this product used to be — while `/you` has led with the score, the character and the four components for a while. A compact dial, the archetype and four component pips now sit above the money in the same order the app puts them in. Illustrative like the rest of the handset, drawn in JSX, and the dial's arc uses `pathLength="100"` with an even dash pair for the same reason the mark does.
- **The marketing field is the second world, and it turns over once: a white editorial hero, then a dark field that runs unbroken from the Wrapped deck to the footer.** It is spoken entirely in `--mk-*` tokens, which live in `styles/tokens.css` and are read only by `app/(marketing)`; the app's tokens never appear on the landing and `--mk-*` never enters the app. The hero is black ink on white. Everything below it sits on `--mk-field`, white ink, with `--mk-violet-soft` for eyebrows, `--mk-green-soft` for money-up, `--mk-red-soft` for money-down and `--mk-amber` for the live and the scarce. Full-strength `--mk-green`/`--mk-red` are reserved for the outliers in a chart, so the ordinary days sit back. Violet and amber gradients belong to the artefacts — Wrapped cards, the early-access tier — never to a section's own ground. The phone mocks are `aria-hidden` illustrations of the app, built in JSX, never screenshots. **The five deck faces wear real card grounds from `stratosphere-01`** rather than the flat gradients that stood in before the cards existed — a landing advertising a Wrapped with none of the Wrapped on it is selling a product from before the one it ships. Three of the five grounds are light cards, so each face sets its own `--deck-ink`, `--deck-scrim` and `--deck-base`, and every internal — track, dot, wave, share — follows `currentColor` rather than a fixed `--mk-bg` tuned against one flat colour. `--deck-base` is not decoration: it is the state the face is in when the art has not loaded, and it is what the contrast probe measures against, because a probe cannot read a background image. The *figures* stay illustrative, as the section's own tail says.
- Tone props follow the tokens: `moss`, `signal`, `ember`, `clay`, `accent`, `neutral`, `ink`. Contributor tones are persisted in Mongo, so read them through `contributorTone()` — pre-existing documents still carry the old `gold`/`violet` spellings.

## Type — one voice for the instrument, voices for the cards

- **General Sans** 400–700 is the product's voice everywhere it speaks: sentences, buttons, inputs, headings, and hero figures. `.disp` takes it at 600.
- **Space Grotesk** 400–700 is now only `--font-display`/`.num` — the tabular figures inside rows and tables, where a column has to line up. `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums`. Never a sentence, never a label.
- **Public Sans** remains the fallback under General Sans and the face the share cards set their ledes in.
- **JetBrains Mono** 400/500/600 owns labels, eyebrows, timestamps, counts and comparatives. Never body copy, never a heading. The moment a metric label renders in the body face the system smears.
- **Anton (`--font-poster`) is the app's display voice, and Playfair (`--font-serif`) is still card-only.** Anton was confined to share cards on the reasoning that a fourth face is how a type system smears. That holds for *sentences* and not for a scoreboard: this product's tagline is a flight recorder for a portfolio, and a cockpit opens on a number you read at a glance rather than parse, and a condensed poster face set enormous is what that looks like. The rules that keep it from smearing anything are stated on `.poster` in `globals.css` — **figures and section heads only, never a sentence, never a label, never a button; 32px is the floor** (Anton is weight 400, so WCAG's large-text allowance barely reaches it and it reads a size smaller than its px value); **one per screen**, which on `/you` is the score at 140px. Playfair is unchanged and enforced by scope.
- Hierarchy comes from size contrast, not new elements: the number huge in the grotesque, the label tiny in mono, the tail a quiet body line. If a card reads flat, widen the gap between number and label — do not add a weight, a colour or an element.
- Three text colours: `--ink` for figures and headlines, `--ink3` for sentences and tails, `--meta` for mono metadata (`--ink2` for mid emphasis where a row needs it).
- **General Sans is the product's voice, declared once**, in `app/layout.tsx` as `--font-voice`, and read by all three groups. Headings and hero figures take it at 600–700, which is what makes a score read like the year on the Wrapped screen. `.num` keeps Space Grotesk with `tabular-nums`, because a ledger column has to line up and that is the whole job of that class. Mono is unchanged. Never Roboto, never Inter.
- **Every face is vendored in `public/fonts` and served from our own origin — nothing is fetched from a CDN, at build time or at runtime.** This is not tidiness. `next/font/google` downloads its woff2 files *during `next build`*, so one bad minute at `fonts.gstatic.com` fails the build; it did, on CI run 31859584940, with "Failed to fetch `Playfair Display`". And General Sans was a `<link>` to `api.fontshare.com` duplicated in three group layouts, which put a third party in the critical render path of every page and handed it every reader's IP — while `/legal/privacy` claims nothing of the sort is there. `npm run fonts` refreshes the files; the paths in `layout.tsx` must stay written-out literals, because `next/font/local` parses that call statically and rejects a shared constant. Satori takes TTF/OTF/WOFF and **cannot read woff2**, which is why `outfit-800.ttf` sits alongside the rest and why share cards had been silently setting their type in a fallback face.

## Screen structure

- **One window on the dashboard, and it is the year.** There were three chips — 45D, YTD, ALL — and two of the three changed the headline figure and nothing else on the page, because the race is always year to date, the score is always tonight's and the set is always this year's. A control that moves one number out of eight is furniture. `RANGE` is a constant in `app/(app)/you/page.tsx` now and the head says which window it is on.
- Every screen is score-first: `<ScreenHeader>` sticky at the top, then a hero number and its decomposition. Nothing above the fold is prose.
- **There is one screen and it is `/you`.** Each block is one idea: an eyebrow, one figure, a sentence, and one idiom. **A block is not a card** — no fill and no border, because a filled plate inside a filled page is the small-rectangles-inside-a-big-one tell, and 96px of nothing between blocks divides them better than a border would.
- **The year is built by the sync and announced on the dashboard.** `/api/snaptrade/sync` calls `warmUser` in `after()` once the response is out, so the deck exists before anybody opens `/wrapped` rather than being built by whoever gets there first — which is the difference between an a-ha inside five minutes and one on the second visit. `<WrappedReady>` is the one notice this product has and it is the first thing on `/you`: the year, the count of what the ledger actually proved, and a door. **It stops.** `wrappedOpenedAt` is written the first time the deck is opened and it never renders again; a banner that keeps announcing something you have seen is an advert.
- **The twelve are ordered by what a fresh account can earn, not by what reads best on a full year.** Cards 1–7 are reachable from a first sync, so a three-day-old account meets a run of real cards rather than stalling at card two and scrolling past nine locked frames. Reordering the array is not the whole change: the generated art in `public/wrapped/2026/art/<set>` is named by number and has to be renamed with it, `wrapped/templates/*.html` regenerated, `DECK_VERSION` in `lib/wrapped/year.ts` bumped so stored decks rebuild, and the landing's five deck faces repointed — they name a *file*, and each carries the ink and base tuned to that particular ground. The acceptance harness finds its forced-failure card **by key**, never by number: pointed at "05" it silently landed on `busiest`, whose template carries neither a figure nor a ticker, so every corruption was a no-op and three open gates reported as three passes.
- **Wrapped is a panel on the dashboard, not a route you have to know about.** `<WrappedPanel>` sits directly under the summary row with the year, the earned count, the first five cards and one door; the deck, its locked frames and the player stay on `/wrapped`. It is the product's most shareable surface and it was the least-visited screen in it. It costs no extra read: `assembleWrappedFrom(data, opens)` takes the screen the page has already loaded, and `assembleWrapped(userId)` is the thin loading wrapper the other callers use. `loadScreen` defaults to the same 400 scores the assembly needs, so the two were literally re-reading one document.
- `<ScreenHeader>` carries a conversational title over a mono line of machine facts, then the account cluster: the sync pill, the tier chip and the one upgrade button. The score rides it on every route so the number never leaves the screen — except on Home, which passes `score={null}` because the ring and the figure are the first thing on the page and a third copy in the chrome is noise. **Investor Age** (computed in `lib/score/age.ts`, deterministic, bounded 18–72, exposure deliberately absent) reads violet wherever it appears.
- **`app/(flex)` is the Wrapped flow and it is not one of the seven — it is its own world.** `/start` and `/wrapped` speak the marketing field and General Sans, carry no rail, and sit outside the launch lock, because the landing hands off to them directly and a signed-out visitor has to be able to reach them.
- **Wrapped is a stacked, swipeable deck. It is never a gallery.** Three builds got this wrong before it stuck: a snap rail showing one and a half cards on a desktop navigated by thirteen anonymous dashes; a cover-plus-grid contact sheet that fixed the navigation and broke the impression; and a flat rail of a dozen cards side by side, which is the same failure in its purest form — at the size a dozen posters fit next to each other, not one of them can be read. A poster is looked at, one at a time. So `<YearDeck>` is a **pile**: the top card at a size you can actually read, the next three behind it as depth, flipped by drag/swipe, arrow keys, the two halves of the screen, or the arrows beside it. "Play your year" opens the same pile full screen. The dashboard's `<YearBlock>` is one pile that links there. The landing's `WrappedDeck` has always been a pile; these are now the same object in the same grammar.
- **A card is framed, never re-implemented.** Each is a finished 1080×1920 document with its own stylesheet, faces and palette, so it renders in an `iframe` with `srcdoc` — the server render already holds the document, and putting twelve fixed stages into the app's cascade would let two vocabularies fight. The frame scales the stage, so every surface is one drawing at a different magnification rather than several layouts to keep in step.
- **The scale variable is a number, not a length.** `scale()` takes a ratio, and `calc(300px / 1080)` is `0.278px` — which does not fail loudly, it invalidates the whole transform. The frames then showed each card's top-left corner at 1:1, and every card's type sits in its lower third, so a deck of posters drew as a row of empty gradients. `--card-w` is unitless and every length derives from it (`calc(var(--card-w) * 1px)`).
- **A deck holds only what was earned; what was not is a list under it.** A deck that is half artefact and half inventory is neither — a reader scrolling their year should not hit a locked tile between two real cards. `<StillToCome>` states the rest as hairline-ruled rows: the frame number, its drawn teaser, its name, and the one condition that mints it. Never a date, never a figure.
- The frame number is the structural device and it is load-bearing: the deck, the full-screen player, the dashboard block and `<StillToCome>` all read `wrapped/cards.mjs` in one order, so a reader can say "nine" and mean one thing.
- **Everything gated carries a drawing of what is behind the gate.** `components/cards/Teaser.tsx` holds one closed vocabulary of drawn readouts — a ring with a gap, four meters, a distribution with a marker, a fanned set of cards — and both surfaces that gate something read from it: the `<StillToCome>` rows under the deck (`CARDS[n].teaser`) and the roadmap rows. **Every drawing is shapes and carries no numerals.** A teaser with a plausible figure on it would undermine every real figure beside it, on the screen whose whole claim is that its numbers came off a brokerage: a ring with a gap says *a score*, it does not say 82. They are drawn rather than fetched for the same reason the rail's route glyphs are — they must be on the first paint and they take `currentColor`. `--teaser-lit` is the one lit colour in a drawing and the caller sets it: violet where the thing is Supercruise's own reading, green where it is money, amber where it is scarce.
- **The roadmap under the deck is a list, not seventeen cards.** `<ComingSoon>` used to give every item the full card treatment — border, blurred drawn readout, lock glyph, name, sentence, status — seventeen times down four bands, which is a wall: nothing is scannable because nothing is distinguishable, and the ghost readouts made a section about *not having data yet* look dense with data. Four groups, one hairline-ruled row each with its own teaser drawing, a status word from a closed set of three, and a small tally of those three. Seventeen rows survive being a list only because every drawing is different — the tiled build reused six shapes across seventeen tiles, which is what made it read as one component repeated — and each group lights its drawings in its own hue so the eye can tell at a glance which section it is in. No dates, ever: a shipped date is a promise that file cannot keep.
- **Setup ends in the dashboard, and the door is only drawn when it opens.** `/start`'s linked state and `<FirstRun>`'s finished state both hand over to `/you` rather than leaving the reader on whichever screen the brokerage portal happened to return to. The greeting and first read still happen on `/wrapped`, because that is where the portal lands and the cards filling in underneath is the whole point of it — the door appears once the sync is done. **Both read `appLocked()` and omit the door while it is shut**: `(flex)` sits outside the launch lock so a signed-out visitor can reach it and `(app)` sits behind it, so a live setup screen can offer a dashboard that silently redirects to the marketing page — connect, sync, press the button, land on the landing with nothing said, which is the exact loop the flag's own documentation warns about. An affordance that will refuse you is absent, not present.
- **"Go to app" is the way in, stated once in `app/(marketing)/GoToApp.tsx` and the same everywhere.** The marketing pages said "Log in", which names a mechanism rather than a destination, and a signed-in reader who landed on the marketing page had no obvious way back into the product — the primary action said "Get started free", which is what you say to someone who has not started. One button now: signed in it is a link to `/you`, signed out it *is* the sign-in, posting to Google and coming back at the dashboard rather than at whichever screen the reader was on. **It renders nothing while `appLocked()`** — a door that is shut is not drawn. The landing's hero swaps its own primary to it when there is a session, and the nav drops the waitlist button below 1180px because three actions do not fit a laptop.
- **Signing in is one click from anywhere.** `<GoogleSignIn>` defaults to `redirectTo="/you"` and appears in the landing nav and the Wrapped bar as well as on the signed-out app screens.
- **One route on the rail: `/you`. `/wrapped` is the subpage its year block opens into, and `/profile` is settings behind the avatar.** Navigation is furniture — with a single screen there is nothing to navigate between, and a rail that pretends otherwise is chrome asking to be read. `MOBILE_ROUTES` is the dashboard plus the account, because below 900px the rail is gone and a one-item tab bar would leave a phone with no way to reach settings, the linked institution or sign-out. It was seven routes, then three. Between them the seven held maybe two pages of content and a great deal of repetition — the archetype rendered on three, the components on two, and a third of the tiles were locked panels showing invented figures. `/home`, `/dna`, `/patterns`, `/insights` and `/cards` are `permanentRedirect`s into `/you`; `/ledger` is a plain 307 because the ledger may return. `/today`, `/portfolio`, `/activity` and `/reports` still redirect too — they are in every bookmark and every link minted before the rename. **Every redirect stub carries `export const dynamic = "force-dynamic"`**: they were statically prerendered, which baked the locked layout's `redirect("/")` into them at build time, so all of them had been answering with the marketing page. `/profile` is settings, reachable from the avatar, and is not a tab.
- **`/you` is the dashboard, and the read leads it.** The order is: the read, then the money, then the set. The read is `<ScoreHero>` — the score at 140px in Anton and gold, the archetype's **character drawn inside the score ring**, the archetype's own line, the live streaks and the personal best as chips, and the four components as arcs you can close across the foot. Then total value, the four ruled stats, the field, the daily columns beside the allocation ring, the cumulative line beside the money calendar, every scored day as a gold grid, the findings beside the Wrapped door, and `<Collection>` — twelve frames and which of them the ledger has proved.

  Account value used to lead, and it is the least interesting true thing this product knows: a brokerage app says the same number faster, and it moves whether or not the reader did anything. What Supercruise knows that nothing else does is what the *conduct* looked like — it has been computing exactly that every night, and drawing it at 64px on a settings page. The whole game layer was built, unit-tested and never mounted: `activeStreaks`, `selfPercentile`, the sixteen drawn characters, the consistency grid. None of this is new arithmetic; it is the wiring.

  **Every part of it is absent rather than defaulted.** `view.read` is null until the first nightly score lands, and an account with holdings but no score falls straight through to the money-first screen it used to be — because `archetypeFor` never returns null, and a confident character with a name beside somebody's own ledger is the single loudest claim this product makes about a person. A streak under its floor, a percentile under five scored days and a personal best nobody has beaten all come back empty. The grid waits for eight scored days, because one lit cell in a field of empties reads as a broken chart rather than as a young account. **The dial is an attitude indicator, and what tilts its horizon is the delta.** An attitude indicator is a ring with a horizon across it whose whole job is to say whether you are level and which way you are going — which is already the shape this hero is drawn in. The distinction is the whole design: the arc says how far along the score is, so a horizon encoding the score too would be the same measurement twice three inches apart, which is the failure `bare` exists to prevent. `pitch` takes `read.delta` and maps it through a floor, a slope and a ceiling: any non-zero delta starts at **4°**, adds 1.1° a point, and stops at 12°. The floor came out of rendering it — the horizon states a *direction*, and a purely proportional tilt makes the commonest case, a delta of one or two, geometrically invisible, so the device says nothing on the days it is most often asked to speak. The ceiling is the other half: a big week must not roll the dial over. A null or zero delta draws a level horizon rather than inventing one. It lives in its own `<svg>` layer because the arc's element is rotated -90° so the arc can start at twelve o'clock, and a horizon sharing that transform would be drawn on its side. It is etching: at 16% and 10% it cannot compete with the arc or the character, and if it ever does, the horizon loses.

  **The character sits inside the ring rather than filling it** — 96px in a 228px dial, down from 132px: the reading is the arc and the numeral, and a mascot pressed against the stroke makes the gauge read as a frame around a portrait.

  **The four bands are one table, in `lib/score/shape.ts`.** The streaks banded a day at 78/62/58 while the heat grid banded it at 90/78/64, so the same Tuesday could be "inside your rules" in the ring and a pale cell in the grid beside it. `scoreBand()` is what both read now. **`EXCEPTIONAL` is 94, not 90**: at 90 a disciplined account put nearly every cell of the year grid at full strength — a hundred and eighty tiles of one colour, which is a fill rather than a texture and is the failure `HeatGrid`'s own note warns about. The top of a four-step ramp has to be rare, or the ramp has three usable steps and a background. The other three edges did not move, so nothing else reading the table changed.
- **`/public` is the handle, drawn as an object.** It was an eyebrow, the words "Coming soon", one sentence and a link into a settings anchor — which is exactly what this file says a tab must never be, a destination that lands you in a form. The name is a card now, with the handle across it and its facts stamped in the corners, and reserving happens underneath it rather than on another screen. **No figure about an account appears on it**: the page is reachable by anyone with the link, so the rule is `/@handle`'s rule. Three drawn panels state the whole contract — what is on a public ledger, what never is, and who can see it — and every drawing is shapes with no numerals, because a plausible figure on the page promising restraint about figures is the worst place in the product to print one. **Reserving is not publishing**: the store keeps `handle` and `publicLedger` as separate fields and this screen only ever writes the first.
- **The insights page says something on day one.** It used to answer with a waiting screen for the whole of the first ten market days: "a pattern read off a few days would be a coincidence with a headline". True of *patterns*, and it made the one screen a new reader is most curious about say nothing on the one day they are most curious. A positions snapshot is a complete present-tense fact the moment it arrives, so `lib/insights/first.ts` reads what needs no history — concentration, industry mix, cash, the longest thing still held, buying cadence, which weekday entries land on. Every one has its own floor and returns nothing under it; **none of them touches P&L**, because a fortnight of P&L is noise, and none compares the reader to anybody. The ten-day gate now applies only to the behaviour reads, and while it is closed the page states what is coming and how far off it is as a real subtraction — never a date.
- **`/holdings` leads with the map.** It was eight columns of right-aligned figures under eight uppercase labels: a correct rendering of the data and an unreadable one at a glance, and nobody screenshots a spreadsheet. The heatmap answers what the account is mostly *in* and what it is doing before a single row is read; then the two ends of the book by return; then the table, with units, average cost and price folded into one mono line under the ticker so the row has four zones instead of eight columns. Nothing was dropped — the hierarchy was.
- **The dashboard is three acts, and it says so.** Eleven plates deep, every one the same near-black rectangle at the same radius with the same padding — read end to end that is eight thousand pixels of undifferentiated evidence, with nothing cresting and nowhere for the eye to land. The page has always been in three parts and never said which: **the read** (what the instrument concluded), **the money** (what the brokerage says), **the year** (what the history proved). `<Act>` is a hairline with a mono label sitting on it, three times down the page. It is deliberately not numbered — a numbered marker is honest where the content is a sequence a reader has to keep their place in, and this is an order of importance. The first act carries `lead`, because `:first-child` cannot see it: the Wrapped notice renders above it when there is one, and the full top margin then opened a hundred and twelve pixels of nothing at the top of the screen.
- **The read has a foot, and it is the only thing on the dashboard that asks for anything.** Every figure on the screen was something to look at and nothing on it was something to *do*, while the one shareable object in the product sat seven thousand pixels further down. The foot carries `<ShareButton type="health">`, which mints through the server — the client names a kind and never supplies contents — and the **closest unearned trophy**, stated as a real subtraction of two counts on file. That second one is the reason to open the screen tomorrow, and it is never a date and never a projection. `trophiesFrom` is pure and reads the screen the page already loaded, so the hook costs no query.
- **A figure appears once per screen, not once per panel.** The return read −48.6% in five places and the realised total read +$35,238 in two, both at 40px inside one scroll. The stat row states them; the panels under it draw them. The cumulative panel, the account-value panel and the daily columns all dropped their hero figures — the curve is what those panels are for, and the number is already said.
- **`/trophies` is the case, and everything in it is a fact the ledger already proved.** The machinery existed and had never rendered: `bestStreaks` (the longest run of each kind ever, as against `activeStreaks`, which reports only what is live and is the right reading for a hero), `scoreBand`'s four edges, the round trips on the derived document, and `archetypeFor` run across every scored night. `lib/trophies.ts` is pure and is the whole of the arithmetic; the page reads the screen it already loads and computes nothing else. **Locked is a state, not a gate** — nothing here is behind a tier, nothing is blurred, and a locked tile states the one condition that earns it plus, where the count is a real subtraction of two things on file, a 3px meter and the pair. A single-event trophy carries no pair, because "0 of 1" says less than its own condition does. The four bands each light in one hue so sixteen near-identical tiles stay scannable, and **the sixteen archetypes are a set rather than a scoreboard**: every one is drawn, an uninhabited one loses its light and keeps its shape, and the count beside each is how many nights read that way. The dimming is on the drawing only — an opacity over the whole tile takes the type with it, and a colour a probe measures at the token while the browser composites it at 40% is the one class of contrast failure the sweep cannot see. The twelve Wrapped frames stay on the dashboard with a door here, because a collection drawn on two screens is a product saying one thing twice. **The case has a front as well as a back**: `nextUp` ranks the unearned by how far along they actually are — days on file against the bar, positions held against the bar — so the page opens on what is closest rather than on a tally and two lists. A single-event trophy has no ratio, so it sorts last rather than at zero, where it would push genuinely close things off a list whose whole point is closeness.
- **The night's written reading is on the dashboard, and for a long time it was on no screen at all.** `generateInsight` runs against Anthropic every night, is held character-for-character to the fact pack by `validateInsight`, and is stored per user per day — and the only reader it ever reached was the notify email. It sits under the archetype line in `<ScoreHero>` now, in `--accent` behind a 2px rule, because that is what this palette gives the product's own voice. `/you` fetches it in the same `Promise.all` as the assembly: normally one indexed read, because `warmUser` fills the row nightly ahead of the reader, and a model call only on the uncached path — a fresh account whose first score landed today, which is exactly what this group's `loading.tsx` exists for. Swallowed on failure, because a dashboard must not fail on a sentence and `fallbackInsight` is a deterministic line rather than a gap.
- **The findings are cards, and the chart is the top of the plate.** They were three rows inside one panel, each with its chart shrunk to a 104px chip beside the sentence it was evidence for — so a reader could see that *something* had been measured and never what. `<InsightCard>` gives each one its own plate: a mono eyebrow naming the kind of measurement, the chart at full width across the top, then the headline and the sentence. The eyebrow names the *kind* and never the finding, because the headline already states the finding. The row shape follows the count — three, two or one — since a third-width card sitting alone in a three-column grid reads as two cards that failed to load. **`WeekdayBars` is full width now and was never drawn at full width anywhere**, which is how the one chart that answers "which day" came to be the smallest mark on every screen it appeared on.
- **The race is bars off a zero line, and everything on it says its meaning twice.** The field is a standing, so the position is drawn: each row wears its place as a numeral in a hairline chip and the reader's is the one inverted chip on the surface. Every lane in the field is `--signal`, which is what this palette gives comparison; the reader's own is money, so it is moss or loss. Direction is structural on top of that — a gained lane is solid, a lost lane is hatched inside a 1px hairline — which is what an accessible chart is meant to carry regardless. The bar is 22px; at the 10px it was, in a 14px lane, the thing being compared was the thinnest mark on the screen. Bars are revealed by `clip-path` rather than `scaleX`, because scaling an outlined, hatched bar thickens its own hairline and shears the hatch. **The reader's own lane ends in the mark's dart nose, cut out of the bar rather than added to it**: the shoulders pull in 9px and the tip sits at exactly 100%, so the extreme of the drawing is the extreme of the figure — a marker appended past the end would be a drawn number disagreeing with the printed one. Both keyframes carry the same five points as the resting shape, because `clip-path` only interpolates between polygons of equal point count.
- **The field is the one surface that measures the reader against anything but themselves, and the reader is always in it.** There is no hedge-fund index in this repository and no key for one, so "hedge funds +14.2%" off a constant is not available and must never become available. What the field holds instead is four funds that exist to replicate hedge-fund strategies (`DBMF`, `QAI`, `MNA`, `BTAL`) and the S&P, each quoted through `indexReturnYtd` — the same provider, the same six-hour cache, keyed by symbol rather than by reader. `lib/market/peers.ts` holds the roster. A fund the provider will not quote is **dropped**, never drawn at zero, and under two rows the block is absent. **Two earlier passes withheld the reader and both were wrong**: the first drew no field at all unless the ledger reached January, so a new account never saw the block; the second drew the field and withheld the reader's row, so a new account watched five funds race without them. Both were avoiding a part-year figure beside a full-year one — which has a better answer than deleting the row. `raceField` takes `{ value, since }`, the reader's row states the day their curve starts whenever it starts late, and the panel repeats it underneath. It is `--signal`, and hue reaches exactly one figure on it: the reader's own, because that one is money.
- **The reader's year-to-date is Modified Dietz, in `lib/returns.ts`, and that is not a detail.** `equitySeries` is portfolio *value*: a deposit lifts it exactly like a gain, so end-over-start next to a fund's return would let anyone out-run the whole field by paying in. `flowsFrom` reads the direction off the transaction's *word* and never its sign — brokerages disagree about whether a withdrawal is a negative amount or a positive amount of a negative kind — and each flow is weighted by how long the money was actually working. With no flow on file it degrades to exactly end-over-start, so an account whose broker reports no transfers reads as it always did. Wrapped card 10 still uses the simpler arithmetic on purpose: that card is about how the account grew.
- **The book is a heatmap, and it replaced the allocation ring.** A pie needs a colour per slice, which is precisely what five hues with one meaning each does not have — so the ring took a ramp of `--signal` and a written exemption from the one-hue-one-meaning rule in order to draw *which* in hue at all. A treemap needs no exemption: **area** says how much of the account is riding on a name, **order** says which, and hue goes back to meaning money — `--moss` up, `--loss` down, `--signal` for a name whose broker reported no cost basis. `lib/heatmap.ts` is the squarify algorithm, pure and tested: tiles come back in percentages so one drawing works at 150px in the hero and full width on `/holdings`, a name with no market value is dropped rather than drawn at zero, and under two priced holdings there is no map. Light is capped at ±25% so a good year and a moonshot are still different colours. **Type on a tile is white and stops at `--ink2`** — a lit tile is a saturated fill rather than a plate, and `--meta` on one reads 3.6:1, which the sweep caught on every width at once. The legend is gone because every tile states its own ticker, share and return, and the `aria-label` states all three for every name whatever the tiles have room for.
- **`/home` merged into it and is now a `permanentRedirect`.** The split was one question with two halves — "how is it going" and "what does my history say about me" — which put the money on one screen and everything explaining the money on another, joined by a quiet link most readers never followed. Merging the screens merged their queries too: the pair had been loading the same ledger twice across two navigations. The header passes `score={null}`, because the read block sets the number at 62px with its own dial a third of the way down the same page.
- **Every group has a `loading.tsx`, and it is a readout rather than a skeleton.** Both app groups are `force-dynamic` and every route waits on Mongo — the dashboard also on the day's first written insight, which is a model call. With no loading file Next holds the *previous* screen until the new one is ready: you tap through, nothing happens for a second and a half, and the only reasonable conclusion is that the tap missed. These were skeletons — plates where plates go, so the arrival moved nothing — which is the stronger argument on a screen that resolves in a blink and the weaker one on a screen that waits on a database and a model call and spends that whole wait showing grey rectangles. `(app)` draws eight bars in the ink ramp, breathing left to right, under *Calculating P&L*; `(flex)` draws two rings turning against each other around a coin, under *Reading your year*. Both are black, both say what is being done, and both stand still and finished under `prefers-reduced-motion`. Still no spinner: a spinner is a machine saying it is busy, and these are the shapes this product deals in. `/debug/loading` renders both side by side behind `DEBUG_PAGE`, because a `loading.tsx` is transient by definition and is otherwise invisible to the sweep.
- **`app/error.tsx` never prints `error.message`.** It is whatever threw — a connection string inside a driver error, a provider's raw response, a stack frame naming a file. Next already replaces it with a generic string in production, so the only reader who ever saw the real one was a developer and the only reader who could ever see a secret one was a stranger. The digest is the thread back to the server log, and that is the right amount to hand out.
- One hero panel per screen. Row dividers inside a panel are `--line-light`, and the last row drops its border.

## Layout and surfaces

- **The app nav is one row at every width.** It used to wrap below 1080px: the brand and tabs took a full-width row, the sync pill and avatar took another, and on a 390px handset that is three stacked rows and about two hundred pixels of chrome before the product says anything — the rule's own comment said a wrapping bar eats a third of a phone screen, and then wrapped it and unstuck it to compensate. One row, still sticky, about 60px. What gives way as the window narrows is the furniture in the order it can be spared: the tabs start scrolling behind a mask, then the sync pill goes at 860px, then the wordmark at 700px. The mark, the tabs and the avatar survive, because those are what a reader navigates with.
- **There is no desktop rail. The chrome is one row.** It was 220px of column holding a single tab and, a thousand pixels lower, the account cluster — and this file already said why that was wrong: navigation is furniture, with one screen there is nothing to navigate between, and a column that pretends otherwise is chrome asking to be read. The mark, the avatar (the door to `/profile`) and `<ModeSwitch>` live in `<ScreenHeader>` now, beside the sync pill, the tier chip and the one upgrade button. The canvas took the width back. **Below 900px `MobileTabs` survives with two tabs**, dashboard and account, because a phone still needs a way to reach settings — and two is the floor, since a one-item tab bar is a label rather than navigation.
- **`.page` states `width: 100%`, and that is load-bearing.** `.canvas` is a column flex container, so the page frame is a flex item — and flexbox stretches an item across the cross axis only when its cross size is `auto` *and neither cross-axis margin is auto*. `margin: 0 auto` is two auto cross-axis margins, so the frame was never stretched at all: it was shrink-to-fit, sized to its own widest content and centred by those margins. On the dashboard that is invisible, because a chart row wants more than the viewport and the fit clamps at full width; on a screen built out of wrapping text and auto-fill grids it collapsed to about 800px in a 1440px window with a third of the display empty either side. A new screen that looks mysteriously narrow is this.
- **Reading screens are one centred column capped at 760px; the dashboard takes 1240px.** The narrow measure is right for a document — DNA, Insights, Patterns, the Ledger — and wrong for the one screen whose job is to show several readings at once: a 760px column centred in a 1440px window left a third of the display empty on either side and pushed everything actionable below the fold. Home widens (`home.module.css .wide`) and lays its panels on a two-column grid where each says for itself whether it needs both (`.span`).
- **Panels read from the left.** They used to centre, which suited a single narrow column of one-line statements and broke the moment two panels sat side by side — a centred lockup floating in a half-width panel has no edge to start from and the eye has to find the beginning of every line twice. Data rows stay left-aligned for the same reason they always did.
- **The read block opens with a lockup, not a stack.** The dial on the left, the score enormous beside it, the delta, the written sentence, then the four components across the foot under a hairline. The ring is drawn `bare` there: the figure is already set at 62px an inch away, and a ring printing it again is the same measurement twice — the arc alone says how far along the number is. It takes `--ring-stroke: var(--accent)`, because the score is Supercruise's own reading rather than money or exposure, and it is the only halo on the screen.
- Flex and grid with `gap` only. No margin-spaced inline siblings. Fixed-count grids use `minmax(0, 1fr)`.
- **Radii are large and the type is big, because the plates are quiet — and they are tokens.** `--r-hero` 40, `--r-panel` 32, `--r-card` 28, `--r-tile` 26, `--r-row` 18, `--r-btn` 14, `--r-control` 12, `--r-pill` 999, stated once in `tokens.css`. They used to be hardcoded per module, which is how one surface ended up at 30px, its stat at 22px and a tile inside it at 20px on the same screen; a hardcoded corner on a surface is now a bug. Chart geometry — a bar's cap, a meter's end — is not a surface and keeps its own literal. On a white plate held by a hairline there is no border weight doing the shaping, so the corner is what gives a surface its character — a 12px radius on a 40px-padded plate reads as a form field.
- **Air is the point.** 48px page padding, 40px panel padding, 34px between panels in the dash grid, 26px inside a panel, 140px at the foot. Everything still sits on a 4px base — 4/8/12/16/20/24/26/32/40/48 — and an off-grid value is a bug, not a taste call. If a screen feels crowded the fix is to take a panel off it, not to shave the gaps.
- **A panel has a ground, and it is drawn to a different brief from a card's.** `npm run dash:art` draws six near-black images into `public/dash` — one per band of the page — and `<Panel art="…">` puts one behind the plate at 0.5 opacity under an `isolation: isolate`. A Wrapped card is a poster whose art is the subject; a panel is the opposite, so every ground is briefed for an **empty middle and no bright areas anywhere**, and the opacity here is the real guarantee — the first number to turn down if a reading ever gets harder to make. A panel with no `art` is exactly the panel that shipped before the mechanism existed.
- **On the dashboard a surface is a fill and nothing else.** Every panel, finding card, collection block and frame carried a 1px `--edge`, and on a black ground that draws a visible rectangle around every block on the page — eight of them stacked reads as a form rather than as a document. The outlines are gone and the fill is the whole separation, which is why `--s1` has to sit a step above the ground rather than at it: a plate is only an object if its own value makes it one, and a shadow does nothing on black. **What keeps an outline is anything a reader can press or that carries meaning** — chips, pills, controls, the race's rank chip, and the semantic chip borders (`--moss-line`, `--signal-line`, `--ember-line`, `--accent-line`, `--loss-line`) — plus the structural rules: row dividers (`--line-light`), the rule above a stat column, the rule above a footer. A hover that used to move a border now moves the fill.
- **A stat is not a box**, and for a long time the shipped one was — a border, a fill and a 22px corner, four of them inside the page, which is the small-rectangles-inside-a-big-one tell in its purest form. A `1px --edge` rule *above* the column does the separating instead: no fill, no border box, and four ruled columns under one enormous figure read as a table of contents for it rather than as four competing cards.
- Blur is chrome, not style: the sticky `ScreenHeader` frosts over the canvas, and `filter: blur()` appears on a locked tile. Nowhere else. No bevels, no inner shadows, no glass panels.
- The one gradient in the app is the fade over the drifting field in `app/(app)/app.module.css`, which only paints in dark mode, and its whole job is to push the artwork almost out of sight.
- 44px hit targets on mobile; interactive rows ≥40px. Err toward whitespace.
- **The phone reads from the left too.** Below 780px the hero stacks — dial, then figure, then sentence — and stays left-aligned, because a centred lockup over a column of left-aligned panels makes the phone look like a different screen from the desktop. The dial halves there (`--ring-size` on `.heroRing`; `ScoreRing` reads `var(--ring-size, var(--size))` so a surface can ask for less than the caller did) rather than spending a third of the fold on a circle that carries no number.

## Data idioms

- One idiom per statement, each legible at 200px wide.
- Full equity curves render on `/dna` only. Never four identical sparklines.
- **The derived document's staleness check is the `ledgerHash`, and it is actually compared.** It was computed, stored, and never looked at while the check compared `transactionCount` alone — so a sync that read a new position snapshot without reading a new transaction, which is every sync on an account that is holding rather than trading, moved the equity curve and nothing noticed. `hashLedger` takes five facts (the count, both ends of the ledger, the snapshot-date count and the newest one) so the probe can be five indexed reads rather than a full scan, and `ledgerHash` is the same function taken off a ledger already in memory. The two must agree for identical input or every page view rebuilds.
- Screens read `lib/db/derived.ts`, they do not scan. Round trips, daily P&L, equity and hold times are materialised once per sync — four screens each rebuilding every round trip per navigation is a timeout waiting for a big enough ledger. Bump `DERIVED_VERSION` when that file changes meaning.
- A name whose FIFO-implied units disagree with the position snapshot is excluded from statistics, never silently averaged in. The position still renders; only the inference stops.
- No decorative gradients and no emoji.
- Icons are a closed vocabulary, not a set. `lib/icons/names.ts` `ICONS` is the whole list, and a new glyph means a new line in it — `/api/icon` serves names, never search terms. They label a fixed taxonomy — the ledger kinds, the three tiers, and the five situations an empty state can be in — and never decorate a heading, a button or a card.
- An empty state's icon names the *situation*, not the screen: signed out, deployment unconfigured, no brokerage, nothing synced, nothing scored yet. The same predicament looks the same on all seven screens.
- `iconsEnabled()` is a server-side read — the key is not `NEXT_PUBLIC` and must not become one. A client component asking it always gets `false`, so a screen asks once and passes the answer down as a prop.
- The rail's route glyphs in `components/app/routes.tsx` are drawn inline by us and stay that way. They are chrome — they must be on the first paint of every screen and take `currentColor` so the active state is one background swap. A fetched glyph would be a downgrade and a dependency.
- An `<Icon>` is painted as a CSS mask, never an `<img>` or inline SVG: the shape comes from the API and the colour is `currentColor`, so a glyph takes the token of the row it sits in and no palette hex enters from outside `styles/tokens.css`.

## Stat card grammar

- Every card reads label → number → unit → why, enforced by `<Stat>` prop order: `eyebrow → value → unit → tail`.
- The eyebrow names the metric the number measures — never an event name over a percentage.
- The tail always ships a specific comparison, never generic encouragement.

## Motion

Motion is the one warm gesture in an instrument aesthetic. The softening happens in time rather than in ornament — do not add a decorative element to compensate.

- Global transition on buttons and inputs: `background, border-color, color, box-shadow` at .16s ease.
- **Scroll-driven reveals, not a load stagger.** Sections carry `data-reveal` and the `rise` keyframe; one rule in `globals.css` retargets them onto the scroll position where the browser supports it. Content above the fold is past its range at load, so it is simply there.
- Meters use `scaleX` at 800ms staggered 70ms; wave bars use `scaleY` staggered 8ms per bar so the chart draws left to right.
- The ring renders at the full-circle `stroke-dashoffset` and gets its real offset in an effect, so CSS transitions it. **The static state must be correct if the effect never runs** — under reduced motion the offset is set immediately.
- A mode change crossfades over .42s via `[data-surface] *`. Flipping light/dark should feel like a dimmer, not a repaint. It also means a contrast probe taken sooner than that measures mid-flight colours.
- Hover: rail items −1px, trophy cards −6px with −.7deg, heat cells scale 1.35, the mode toggle rotates 45°. Panels do **not** lift on hover — with borders gone there is nothing to lift.
- Everything honours `prefers-reduced-motion`, and JS tweens check `matchMedia` before starting.
- Marketing may use scroll-driven motion (`animation-timeline: view()` / `scroll()`), always behind `@supports` and `prefers-reduced-motion`, and always so the static state is the finished design. Never write a hover rule against a property a scroll animation also writes — `fill: both` wins and the hover silently dies.
- Ease-out, nothing over 500ms — for *arrivals*. Ambient loops (`breathe`, `floaty`, `wander`, `shimmer`) run long and slow, and are allowed in exactly two places: light behind live data (the score ring's halo) and marketing surfaces. Never on a panel, never on text a reader is parsing.
- Arrivals are staggered, not simultaneous: header pills, money rows and heat cells land oldest-first with delays under 300ms total. A grid caps its cascade so scale never slows arrival.
- Count-ups run on `setInterval`. The real value is the default render — if the tween never starts, the number on screen must still be correct.
- **`<CountUp>` is mounted, and for a long time it was not.** It sat in `components/idioms` fully written and unit-tested with no caller while every hero figure on the dashboard rendered as a static string. The score, the daily P&L total, the cumulative total and the account-value substitute all count now. It tweens *backwards into place* — the initial state is the finished, formatted number and the effect drops to zero and walks up — so a figure this product cannot afford to print wrongly is never something an animation has to arrive at to become true.
- **A line chart draws itself, and the finished state is a zero offset.** `PnlWave` and `EquityCurve` set `pathLength={1}`, so one dash unit is the whole path and the offset is a share of it — no length arithmetic, and a curve whose animation never starts renders complete. The area under each fades in behind the line rather than with it.
- **Arrivals are directional where the direction means something.** The four stat columns draw their rules left to right; the race settles from the front of the field back, so the reader's row lands last on a chart about where they placed; the twelve collection frames cascade at 28ms capped at 300ms, so a longer roster would not slow the last one down.
- **One hover on the dashboard, and it is the findings.** An insight card lifts 4px and its edge comes up. Panels still do not lift — with borders gone there is nothing on them to lift — but a finding is a thing the reader is meant to want to read.
- Everything honours `prefers-reduced-motion`.

## Copy rules, enforced in review

- **Borrowed vocabulary is not a description.** The landing said "Transform the way you trade" over "Whoop for your portfolio" — a product that never tells anybody to trade, described by naming a fitness band. The preview card beside it printed "Strain 14.2", which measures nothing this repository computes. Say what the thing does in its own words, or the marketing is selling a different product from the one that ships.
- **A figure appears once per card.** The drawdown finding stated its realised P&L in the sentence while every surface drawing a finding already prints `impact` as the hero and again on the evidence line — one number, three times, and a two-line reading that reads as a paragraph.
- **A stepper shows one step's body.** Four steps each carrying a paragraph is four paragraphs in a section whose argument is "one thing a day". The titles are the sequence; `grid-template-rows: 0fr → 1fr` opens the active one with no measured height and no JS, and the static state is step one open.
- **No peer claim anywhere it is not locked.** A percentile needs a population this product does not have. Every in-app surface refuses to print one and draws a `<LockedCard>` instead — so the landing advertising "top 3% of investors trading like you" was selling the one figure the product will not compute. `selfPercentile` is real: where today sits among the reader's own scored nights, and it says whose distribution it is.
- **Agreement is computed, never assumed.** `institutionLine` hard-coded "is", so anybody who linked two brokerages read "Interactive Brokers and Fidelity is connected" on the screen this product most wants to sound sure of itself on.


- Descriptive, never prescriptive. No "you should", no "consider", no urgency.
- No exclamation marks. No coaching tone. Sentence case everywhere.
- Buttons name what happens ("Recompute score", never "Submit"). Card headings say something rather than labelling.
- Empty states name the next move, never "nothing here yet".
- Banned: supercharge, unleash, 10x, revolutionize, game-changing, seamless, effortless, "AI-powered" as a headline, growth hacking, "in seconds".
- Numbers describe behaviour — never a benchmark and never another person — except in explicit percentile surfaces, which are `--signal`.
- One notification a day. Never a price alert.

## Code conventions

- Next.js App Router. Server components by default; `'use client'` only where interaction requires it.
- `lib/score`, `lib/engine` and `lib/cards` are pure functions — no I/O.
- **One paid plan, and it only sells what a server route actually checks.** Gating lives in `lib/tiers.ts` and nowhere else: components ask `can(user, capability)`, and no component reads `user.tier` directly. There were two tiers and twelve capabilities, six of which had no implementation at all — a paywall whose feature list is longer than its feature set is the one thing a paywall must never be. `Tier` is `free | pro`; `Capability` is the five the routes check (`reportCarousel`, `correlationCard`, `publicationExport`, `transparentExport`, `liveBadge`). `Capability` has no member for minting, sharing or rarity, so those gates cannot be written — sharing is never paywalled, and `canMintCards()`/`canShareCards()` take no tier on purpose. **Every read of a stored tier goes through `normaliseTier()`**, which maps the older `plus`/`trader` names forward: a rename in this repo must not drop a paying subscriber to free. Stripe is the source of truth; `tierFromStatus()` downgrades to free unless the status is live. There is one tier module — `lib/billing/tiers.ts` was a second, dead but tested, disagreeing with the live one on both members and signature, and it is gone.
- **What Pro buys has a surface.** The four export capabilities were enforced in API routes nothing in the app linked to, so the plan was a feature list with no button. They are a hairline-ruled block at the foot of `/you`, live on Pro and stating the plan name when not — no blurred figure, because a plausible number under a blur is the one thing this product must not print.
- **`/pricing` states the plans off `FREE_ALWAYS` and `CAPABILITY_LABEL`** rather than a hand-written list, so the page cannot drift from what the gates enforce, and starts a checkout in place for a signed-in free account. It lives in `(marketing)`, wears the shared `Chrome` nav and footer, and speaks `--mk-*`. Every lock links there; nothing opens a modal.
- **The trial is thirty days from the brokerage connecting**, not from account creation — an account with no ledger has nothing to unlock and would burn the window on an empty screen. It states an end date once, where the plan is described. No countdown, no nag. **The offer is stated where the decision is made**: it was on the landing and on `/pricing` and nowhere on `/start`, which is the one screen that asks somebody to hand over a brokerage — so the reader most likely to hesitate was the only one never told what it costs. `/start`'s facts and the dashboard's connect and signed-out empty states all say thirty days free, no card, and name the anchor, because a month that starts at sign-up is a month somebody spends on an empty screen.
- Monetization is present, never blocking. A locked tile occupies the slot its unlocked twin would, blurred, with the tier named — never a modal wall in front of a screen the user asked for. Every lock carries **readiness computed from real sample counts** (`Ready now` / `38 tags to go`); never fake a Ready. No countdown, no discount banner, no trial nag.
- **The minted block on `/you` shows the cards, through `<TrophyCard>`.** It projected a `title` field that does not exist on a card document — the label is `label` — so every row fell through to the kind slug and the block rendered a list of internal names beside truncated URLs. `TrophyCard` also carried a locked variant that blurred a card behind a "category" paywall and a "Held by N% of members" line; the first is a gate `Capability` deliberately has no member for, the second a claim about a member base nothing measures. Both are gone, and its rarity vocabulary is now the store's own — `rare` or nothing, worn as the same word the card itself wears.
- **A locked card's share button is absent, not disabled.** Never show a user an affordance that then refuses them.
- `<ShareButton>` is the one share affordance and it mints through `/api/cards/mint`: the client names a kind and never supplies contents, so a crafted request cannot mint a card claiming a number that never happened. What comes back is a 96-bit slug, which is the card's whole access model. `/card/[type]/[id]` is the typed URL and 301s to `/c/[slug]`, where the OpenGraph tags live — two dynamic names cannot share a path segment, which is why there is no `/og/[type]/[id]`.
- Share cards are public by slug: `cardBySlug` takes no userId and projects only what a card renders. The slug is 96 bits of randomness, because it is the card's entire access model.
- Two model providers, split by surface rather than by medium. **Anthropic writes the product's own voice** — the daily insight, the one sentence the instrument says about you. **OpenAI serves the artefact**: it drew the twelve Wrapped backgrounds and the sixteen archetype avatars, and it writes the one caption on each Wrapped card. The split used to be prose-versus-pictures and it is not any more, so the rule is now where the words appear: an app screen's sentences come from Anthropic, a card's caption comes from OpenAI. **`/legal/privacy` names both and states exactly what each is sent, and it changes in the same commit as any change to that.**
- **A figure on a Wrapped card is named by a label and defined by its caption.** `measures` in `wrapped/cards.mjs` is the mono line above the hero and it is `null` only where the hero names itself (the year, an archetype). Without it the biggest number on a card has nothing naming it: card 07 set the *year's* trade count under a heading reading "Most active month", so the figure read as the month's and no line on the card said otherwise. The caption then says what was counted and over what period — plainly, in at most twelve words and with **no numeral of its own**, because every other figure on the card is checked against the stats character for character and one inside a caption is the only one nobody is comparing to anything. The three written fallbacks per card and the model's brief are held to the same standard; captions used to be moods ("Everything you did, added up"), which read identically on any card.
- **A ticker on a card wears its company's mark, and the slot is drawn.** `.logo` in `card.css` is a finished plate; `stampLogos` points its `<img>` at `/api/logo/<symbol>` on the way to a screen, so a card rendered where that route cannot be reached — a PNG with no origin, a fresh deployment — shows the plate rather than a hole. The address is stamped at render for the same reason `fitHero`'s size is: `src="/api/logo/{{BEST_TICKER}}"` would fill an *attribute*, which `normalise` does not blank, so every model-written card would fail the markup check. The mark is a **sibling** of the ticker, never a child — nested, it would become part of the ticker's own text and fail the value check.
- **A Wrapped card's caption is the only thing a model contributes to it.** `lib/wrapped/stats.ts` computes every figure and formats it into the exact display string; `fill` substitutes; `validate` compares what came back to the stats character for character and throws the whole answer away on any difference, falling back to one of three written captions per card. A card is sent only its own tokens (`statsFor`), never the whole set — the drawdown card has no business being written with the reader's name and best ticker in the context window. `WRAPPED_MODEL_MODE=full` hands the model the template as the pipeline was specified; the default `caption` mode asks only for the sentence, because a validated `full` answer is by construction identical to the deterministic one and its only remaining freedom is to fail.
- **The card stylesheet is self-contained and may not cite an app variable.** `wrapped/templates/card.css` declares its own `Voice`, `Machine`, `Poster`, `Serif`, `Grotesk`, `Geometric` and `Lede` families off the vendored faces. `--font-voice` and `--font-mono` are set by `next/font` in the root layout, and a card renders standalone — as a page, in a frame, into a PNG — so they resolve to nothing there; an unresolved `var()` inside `font-family` does not fall through to the next name in the list, it invalidates the whole declaration, and every line on the card sets in the browser's default serif. It did, on all twelve, and only rendering them caught it. `npm run wrapped:check -- --render` probes each drawn card for the family that took, the face that loaded, and whether any line of the lockup crossed the stage — the last one is the only check in the file that looks at the drawing rather than at the markup.
- **`fitHero` sizes a card's hero from the characters it holds, in the face it holds them in, at render and not at fill.** A viewport clamp cannot know whether the value is `129` or `+42.8%`, so both were set at 380px and the wider one ran off the edge for every reader whose return had a sign and a decimal. It was one constant — 0.555em a glyph — while there was one hero face and it was monospaced; with seven faces, five of them proportional, `111` and `%%%` are no longer the same width. So the advances are **measured** per face and per character by `npm run wrapped:metrics` and committed as `wrapped/metrics.mjs`, and the fit adds them up. It runs in `cardDocument`, on the way to a screen, so the stored card stays character-identical to what came through the gate and a font-size can never become a place a wrong number hides. The CSS clamp is the floor under a card that never went through the fit, never the sizing — its middle term is `34vw`, which on a fixed 1080 stage is a constant.
- **A component with no evidence is null, and a score is built only from what was measured.** Every one of the four used to carry a neutral fallback — consistency 72, patience 72, exposure 88, adherence about 78 — so an account that connected, held eight positions and traded nothing scored **76 with all four above the bar**, was told "all four components sit above the line at once", and was handed a confident archetype off a ledger that had proved none of it. Every number in that reading was a constant in this repository. `computeScore` now returns `null` under two measured components and the night is not stored; above the floor the weights renormalise over what *is* measured, `measured` travels with the row, and the screen says "scored on 3 of 4" with an em dash on the arcs it could not read. **A default is not a measurement; it is a number wearing one's clothes**, and a reader cannot tell them apart.
- **`archetypeFor` returns null unless all four components are measured.** A corner of a four-bit cube needs a value on every axis, and a missing component used to read as "below the bar" — which sorted every thin account into The Improviser and every lucky default into The Composed. Neither was a reading. It is also the one thing in this product that goes beside a person's name, so it is the last claim that may be made from a default.
- **The basis may move, and it is never silent.** A day that makes a previously unmeasurable component measurable changes what the score stands on, and renormalising over three instead of two is a real change rather than a nudge — so the anti-gaming test asserts stability *on the same basis* and asserts that a basis change shows up in `measured`. The old test ran against a six-trade fixture where one extra buy doubles the month's trade count, which is a genuine change of pace and was being hidden by a constant.
- The sixteen archetypes in `lib/archetypes.ts` are the corners of a four-bit cube — each score component is above the fixed bar (60) or it is not — so nothing is curated to reach a round number and every profile lands in exactly one. The bar is fixed rather than relative to the reader's own mean, because an archetype goes on a share card and has to mean the same thing whoever wears it. No model is involved.
- **Archetype avatars are sixteen cartoon characters, drawn by us in `lib/avatars/drawn.ts`, and that file is what ships.** An archetype is the one thing in the product that goes beside a person's name, and the two earlier passes — thin line marks, then neon glyphs from an image model — both read as instrumentation. Each character is built out of the abstract form named in `ARCHETYPES[n].emblem`, so the table stays the single description of what an archetype looks like, and moss still carries the reading: more moss in a character is more components above the bar. The face is two punched dots and a mouth — a rendered human face beside someone's name would be a claim about who they are. `public/archetypes` is empty and `lib/avatars/manifest.ts` is generated from it (`npm run avatars:manifest`), never hand-edited; a PNG dropped there by `npm run avatars` overrides the drawn character for that key, so `lib/avatars/prompt.ts` asks for the same flat cartoon mascot and never names the archetype — a model given the word "Sentinel" draws a guard.
- **Card art is thirteen fixed images, one per kind**, authored once by `npm run backdrops` and committed to `public/cards`. Nothing generates at request time and nothing is stored per card. This is the Wrapped model: a template is designed once and worn by millions, and what makes a card someone's own is composited over it in type — their figure, their sentence, their company. Minting is instant and free.
- `lib/wrapped/brief.ts` and `scenes.ts` are pure and exist to *author* those thirteen, not to run per user: four measured quantities (magnitude, direction, texture, density) taken from a representative ledger shape each scene, so the picture for a kind matches what that kind is about.
- **In-app card artwork is drawn, and a photograph is the ground under it.** `components/cards/Artwork.tsx` holds twelve flat hard-edged figures, one per kind, and behind each sits one Unsplash photograph — desaturated, luminosity-blended and washed to the card's hue, so it reads as material rather than as a subject. Both halves are finished on their own: a kind with no photograph wears the drawn geometry alone, which is the design and not a gap. The queries in `lib/unsplash/query.ts` are the sky now — cirrus, a cloud deck, a contrail, a plume, ice refraction — and the rule they were always written under is what makes that safe: **atmosphere, never aviation as a subject**. No cockpits, no airports, no aircraft portraits, no people; a photograph of someone's life makes a claim about the reader the ledger never made.
- **Two cards wear the photograph across the whole face, and the list is `FULL_GROUND` in `WrappedCard.tsx`: `wrapped` and `archetype`.** They are the identity cards — the year, and who the year says you are — and neither carries a figure or a chart that a busy ground would make harder to read. A card on a full ground paints no band backing and no second copy of the picture (`<Artwork onGround>`), because two exposures of one photograph at two opacities is a seam, not a composition. Everywhere else the band stays a band.
- Photographs come from Unsplash through `lib/unsplash`, split the way the icon layer is: `query.ts` is pure and tested (queries, referral parameters, credit, CDN sizing), `fetch.ts` is the only caller. **The store holds the URL, never the bytes** — their terms require hotlinking from their CDN — plus the credit that has to travel with it. Filling it is deliberate and manual: `npm run photos`, or `POST /api/photos/refresh` behind `PHOTOS_REFRESH_TOKEN`. **A page only ever reads the store**, so no amount of traffic can spend quota, and re-running the fill re-picks every photograph under artefacts people have already posted. `/legal/photos` names each photographer and links both them and Unsplash with the referral parameters their guidelines require; like `/legal/icons` it reads the store rather than calling the API.
- **The company is the per-person half.** A card about a position wears that instrument's mark and ticker through `<Logo>`, and the Wrapped card leads with the three names you closed the most round trips in — ranked by count, never by P&L, because ranking a retrospective by profit turns it into a leaderboard.
- The model never draws a glyph. Every figure on a card is set in type by us; a generated number is one nobody can correct, on an artefact whose whole claim is that its numbers came from a brokerage.
- The art prompt (`lib/wrapped/prompt.ts`) is pure and tested. It forbids text, numerals, logos, people and objects, and asks for a quiet centre — the card's own type is set in Outfit by us, and a model drawing a number would be both wrong and unfixable.
- **Company marks come from a chain, and only the first link needs a key.** `lib/logos/sources.ts` is pure and tested: logo.dev when `LOGO_DEV_TOKEN` is set, then Financial Modeling Prep, then parqet — both keyless. Every mark used to come from logo.dev alone, so the whole feature was one unset variable away from silently degrading, and it did: the deployed app rendered a drawn monogram on every holding, every ledger row and every share card. A monogram is the answer for a ticker nobody has a mark for, not the house style. A source is only believed if it answers with an image type **and** enough bytes to be a drawing — both keyless sources answer an unknown ticker with a 200 and a near-empty body, so the status code alone would store a placeholder as a logo. Everything is proxied through `/api/logo/[symbol]`, never a direct URL: the token stays server-side and the route always answers with an image, so no component handles a broken one.
- Marks are stored in Mongo by `{symbol, size}` and the bytes are stored, unlike the photographs — Unsplash's terms require hotlinking, logo providers' do not, and a holdings table renders twenty marks at once. Misses are stored too, or one obscure holding would call three third parties on every page view; a miss expires after a week so a newly listed symbol fills itself in, and a hit never does.
- Icons come from The Noun Project through `/api/icon/[name]`, signed with two-legged OAuth 1.0a in `lib/icons/noun.ts` (pure and tested; the secret never leaves the server). Fetched once and stored in Mongo with no TTL — an icon does not change, every call costs quota, and their terms forbid redistribution, so the route must never accept a search term. Third-party SVG is sanitized before it is stored. Icons are CC-BY: `/legal/icons` names the creators, and it reads the store rather than calling the API.
- Market data (Finnhub, `lib/market`) backchecks the brokerage and never becomes an event. A quote may only correct a mark the last sync left stale — never replace a mark from today, and never surface a move on its own. There is no price alert and no place to write one. `resolveMark` also refuses a quote that disagrees with the broker by more than half: that is a symbol resolving to a different instrument, not a correction.
- Anything a screen shows from market data says so, in mono, via `provenanceLine`. The brokerage stays the account of record: names fill a gap the broker left empty and never overwrite one it gave.
- `lib/market/marks.ts` and `holdings.ts` are pure and tested; `client.ts` owns the network. Calls go through `cached()` — a shared Mongo collection swept by a TTL index, keyed by request and not by user, because the rate limit is per key. Fan-out is bounded by `pooled()`. Every failure path returns the brokerage's own numbers rather than an error.
- `app/og/[slug]/render.tsx`, `lib/logos/fetch.ts`, `lib/email/render.ts`, `lib/wrapped/prompt.ts` and `lib/avatars/prompt.ts` are the files allowed to repeat palette hexes, and the reason is the same in all five: an image at a URL and a message in an inbox both carry no stylesheet, and a prompt describing a colour to a model cannot cite a custom property. Keep the values in step with the `--share-*` tokens, and give every multi-child element an explicit `display`. Their tests (`prompt.test.ts`) necessarily repeat the values they assert. `app/api/badge/[slug]/route.ts` is in the same class — a self-contained SVG at a URL carries no stylesheet. So is `themeColor` in `app/layout.tsx`: a browser chrome colour is a meta tag, and a meta tag cannot cite a custom property, so it is the one place `--bg`'s value is written twice and the two have to be kept in step by hand. `components/cards/StoryViewer.module.css` is the one component allowed `#000`/`#fff`: a fullscreen story player is a lightbox, and a lightbox is black with white chrome whatever the theme does. The landing is `app/(marketing)/page.tsx` + `landing.module.css` plus the shared `Chrome`, `WrappedDeck`, `FirstWeek`, `PnlChart` and `WaitlistForm`: a white hero — two phone mocks apart, the equity line curling across the gap to the Wrapped handset, annotation bubbles on their edges — over a dark field carrying the Wrapped deck, the coming-soon score, the behaviour reads, the first week and the waitlist. Nothing there escapes the `--mk-*` tokens. Every animated piece is a client component whose **static state is the finished design**: the deck renders the full pile with card one on top, `FirstWeek` renders step one selected, and both refuse to start their timer under `prefers-reduced-motion`. Any manual move — drag, arrow, dot, step — stops the autoplay for good rather than fighting the reader for control. The waitlist posts to `/api/waitlist` (idempotent upsert by lowercased email; the client never writes anything but an email and a tier name). **There is no invented social proof and there must not be one again** — "Loved by 320K investors · 4.9 rating" over three drawn faces, and "12,408 investors, same 45 days", were on the page on the launch day of a product with no users; a fabricated review count is a bad idea on any landing and a liability on a financial one. The hero pill keeps the stacked-circle treatment and fills it with broker marks instead of faces — "Robinhood, Fidelity, Schwab and 20+ more", the coverage `/start` already states rather than a new claim invented for the hero, and the actual first question a visitor has. The sample ledger behind `PnlChart` and the phone mocks is illustrative and says so in its own tail. The nav and footer are `Chrome.tsx`, shared with `/pricing`, and the landing is `force-dynamic` because a statically prerendered page freezes its `APP_LOCKED` read at build time.
- Engine findings are descriptive and self-silencing: below their sample floor they return `null` rather than report a coincidence, and the evidence line must never contradict the sentence it sits under. A finding's `impact` is the realised P&L of the pattern's own bucket, summed off the round trips — never a projection, never an extrapolation; where no honest bucket exists (a ratio, a sizing multiple) it stays `null` and the screen shows no figure. Ledger-only findings are materialised in `derived.findings` per sync; tag-joined ones are computed live on Patterns because tags move without a sync.
- **`components/idioms` is a closed set and an idiom with no surface is deleted, not kept warm.** `WaveChart` (a mirrored wave, superseded by `ZeroBarChart` — this chart is money, so the columns hang off a zero line), `SegmentRing` (`ScoreRing` does the job) and `HourHeat` (an hour-by-weekday grid nothing computes, and one whose cells would carry single-digit samples on a retail ledger — which the engine's own sample floor would refuse to report) are gone. A component that renders nothing is a component nobody is maintaining against the tokens, and it reads as a feature to whoever finds it next.
- **The daily pulse is retired.** It asked how the market felt, four taps, and nothing ever read the answers — a second input loop competing with the tag loop, which is *the* input a brokerage cannot supply. Asking someone a question you never use is worse than not asking. The `pulses` collection stays declared and stays in `PERSONAL_COLLECTIONS` so erasure still covers anything written while it was live; nothing writes to it now.
- Screens are assembled from `components/primitives`, `components/idioms`, `components/app` and `components/cards`; new visual patterns start there, not inline.
- The tag loop (`lib/tags.ts`, `<TagPrompt>`) is the only input a brokerage cannot supply and every correlation is downstream of it, which is why it sits second on Home rather than in settings. Two taps, no text field — a free-text reason cannot be grouped, and a reason that cannot be grouped cannot become a finding. `TagDoc` persists the reason lower-cased, so go through `whyKey()`/`whyLabel()` rather than comparing strings.
- Screen derivations live in `app/(app)/derive.ts` and are pure. Nothing there invents a figure: where the ledger cannot answer, the function returns an empty result rather than a plausible one.

## Operations

- **`.env.example` is the contract**, and every integration degrades gracefully when its variables are missing. That is load-bearing rather than polite: CI builds with no secrets at all, so a build that needed a key to compile would be a regression.
- **Two gates, deliberately not one flag, and they point opposite ways.** `APP_LOCKED=1` *shuts* the product; `DEBUG_PAGE=1` *opens* `/debug`, which names every secret, prints the raw ledger and carries live Sync and Score buttons. They were once the same flag, which meant launching the product published that page. The launch gate is open by default now: it was `APP_UNLOCKED=1`-to-open, and a default-closed gate ships every fresh deployment shut — where a shut door is not an error page but a silent 307 back to the marketing site. A second host sat like that for days. Closing is the deliberate act; `APP_UNLOCKED=0` still shuts it for deployments carrying the older name.
- **The nightly jobs sweep, they do not loop.** Both used to iterate every connected user serially inside one request under a 60-second cap — about two accounts before the platform killed it, and everyone behind the cut silently unscored. `lib/db/cron.ts` gives them a stored cursor: each call takes the next slice, stops *starting* users before its budget runs out, and advances past a user who threw rather than parking the table behind them. `closesSweep()` is pure and tested, because wrapping early skips people and never wrapping stops the job while it still answers 200. The schedule is a Railway cron service running `npm run cron`; `vercel.json` declared crons on a platform this app does not deploy to and never fired once.
- **Export and erasure are buttons on `/profile`, not a support address.** Both are defined off one list in `lib/db/account.ts`, and a test asserts every collection in the store is either erased or explicitly exempt with a stated reason — a delete that misses a collection while the export still shows it is a promise broken silently. Deletion cancels a live Stripe subscription first: emptying our mirror would leave one billing that nobody in the product can see.
- **`/legal/privacy` and `/legal/terms` are written against the code**, not from a template. Every claim in them is checkable in this repository — including that the fact pack sent to Anthropic carries no name, no email, no ticker and no dollar figure. If that stops being true, the page changes in the same commit. `SUPPORT_EMAIL` is the address they print.
- **`metadataBase` is required, not decorative.** Without it the `opengraph-image` convention emits a relative path no unfurler can fetch, which on a product whose entire pitch is that people post it is a broken feature. `lib/site.ts` resolves the origin the same way `lib/origin.ts` does — `APP_URL` first.
- **`npm run typecheck`, `npm run lint` and `npm test` all pass, and CI runs them plus the build.** ESLint's config turns off `no-img-element` on purpose: every `<img>` here points at a URL whose bytes Next must not touch, and Unsplash's terms *require* hotlinking from their CDN, so `next/image`'s proxy would be a licence violation rather than an optimisation.
